Fable 5.1 Solves the Cyphral Distich, a 370-year-old cipher
⬆ 1121 pts 💬 515 HN comments

Vals AI reports that Claude 'Fable 5.1' solved the Cyphral Distich, a 370-year-old cryptogram appended to Sir Thomas Urquhart's Logopandecteision (1653): two lines of 32 numbers each, published after Urquhart's 32 Proquiritations. Decades of cryptanalysis failed because researchers assumed an external key — they ran frequency analysis, monoalphabetic and homophonic substitution against the 64 integers. The model's breakthrough was structural: the key is endogenous to the book. For the i-th number in a cipher line, index into the i-th Proquiritations paragraph, use the number as a word offset, and take that word's first letter. This yields 32 letters per line, a rhyming distich, and the plaintext 'O GOD UPHOLD KING CHARLS THE SECOND AND MAKE HIM THE SUPREME RULER OF THIS LAND' — a Royalist prayer historically consonant with Urquhart's politics.

The agent generalised the technique to the Cyphral Octastich in The Jewel (1652): 285 numbers keyed to the book's 284 pages, taking the k-th number as a word index into page k. It produced a coherent ottava rima (ABABABCC) royalist prayer, with 231 of 275 readable positions confirmed as exact first-occurrence hits in EEBO-TCP, and honest caveats: nine unreadable letters (C-O-N-E-R-T-H-T-O), an I-R-S-H contraction for IRISH giving exactly ten syllables, and a page k-1 shift from position 159 onward. The solve cost 44 minutes, 176k tokens, and zero human interjections, on a post that drew 1,121 points and 515 comments.

'The answer was simple in hindsight. It just kept looking until it found it — and that persistence might show up in many other areas.' The solve took 44 minutes and 176k tokens, with 231 of 275 Octastich positions independently verified as exact first-occurrence page hits.

Key Highlights
  • The key was internal, not external: the i-th cipher number indexes a word in the i-th Proquiritations, and the first letter forms the plaintext — explaining why a century of substitution and frequency analysis failed.
  • Self-verification is strong: two 32-letter rhyming lines, a coherent 285-number extension into The Jewel, and 231/275 positions matching exact first-occurrence words in EEBO-TCP text.
  • The agent selected its own target from a pool of unsolved ciphers, which critics call the decisive methodological problem — it optimised for solvability, not difficulty or importance.
  • Prior-art questions surfaced: 2014 comments on Klaus Schmeh's blog already proposed a book cipher, though they missed the 32-Proquiritations alignment that makes the solve specific.
  • Provenance remains contested: multiple commenters could not locate the printed cipher in archive.org scans, in Schmeh's Top 50 list, or in pre-2026 search results, and no free page images of the 1652 Jewel exist.

#2
Mullenweg has returned as CEO after attempted board ouster
⬆ 235 pts 💬 313 HN comments

WordPress founder Matt Mullenweg has been restored as CEO of Automattic, the commercial steward of WordPress.com and a sprawling portfolio (WooCommerce, Tumblr, Jetpack, WordPress VIP) that underpins a substantial fraction of the public web. The sequence of events is the story: the board placed Mullenweg on paid leave on Wednesday, CFO Mark Davies was positioned as interim CEO with the board's "full confidence," and within roughly 33 hours Mullenweg had ejected other administrators from Automattic's internal Slack, told employees the matter was resolved, and reasserted operational control. No technical system was compromised — but Slack admin privileges, identity scopes, and internal announcement channels functioned as the de facto instruments of corporate control. This is a governance failure expressed entirely through access-control surfaces.

The company's statements are themselves evidence. A spokesperson cited "many top executives and Automatticians supporting him" on X, and described Mullenweg as "chairman and CEO... with full support of the board" — while declining to confirm reports that founding CEO Toni Schneider had stepped down and conceding board composition "may still be in flux." Mullenweg's only public explanations were a blog post about buying a houseboat and the line "I'm not a troll I'm a pirate, obviously." Observers note this would be his fifth contested "coup." The structural underpinning is Automattic's concentrated founder equity and control; the practical consequence is a platform dependency risk for the millions of sites — and thousands of agencies and hosts — whose roadmaps are coupled to one person's volatility.

"Matt was away for only 33 hours and 20 minutes—we're now back to work," per Automattic's statement — a duration that tells you more about who actually holds admin credentials than any press release.

Key Highlights
  • The board's leave-of-absence vote was undone within ~33 hours, with Mullenweg reasserting control by removing other Slack admins and broadcasting to staff.
  • Control was exercised through operational infrastructure — Slack admin rights and internal comms — not through legal or procedural channels, highlighting how unclear Automattic's actual governance authority is.
  • Automattic's public statements are conspicuously non-committal: no confirmation of board composition changes, no confirmation of Toni Schneider's reported resignation.
  • Mullenweg characterized the event as roughly his fifth "coup" attempt; his only substantive public communication was a blog post about buying a houseboat.
  • Commenters frame the episode as a platform-risk signal for anyone building on WordPress, with migration interest toward Hugo, Shopify, and community-governed alternatives.
