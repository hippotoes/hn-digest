Un-0: Generating Images with Coupled Oscillators
⬆ 155 pts 💬 36 HN comments

Un-0 introduces a paradigm shift in generative modeling by replacing traditional neural network layers with a simulated system of coupled oscillators, specifically Kuramoto oscillators. The model learns a coupling matrix and natural frequencies that govern the dynamics of thousands of oscillators, whose collective phase evolution towards a fixed point is decoded into an image. On ImageNet 64×64, Un-0 achieves an FID of 6.74, matching the quality of early conventional generators like NCSN and BigGAN when they were first published, albeit with a smaller parameter count. The approach is motivated by the long-term goal of running AI on physical substrates (e.g., analog CMOS) that exploit natural dynamics, promising up to 1000× energy efficiency by letting physics do the computation instead of digital GPUs.

While currently simulated on conventional hardware, Un-0's open-source release enables community experimentation. Ablation studies confirm that the learned dynamics contribute significantly beyond random feature extraction: the model's performance degrades when dynamics are frozen or replaced by a decoder-only baseline. However, scaling remains a challenge—the coupling matrix is O(N²) in oscillator count, raising concerns about practically generating high-resolution images. Comments on Hacker News reflect both excitement for the unconventional approach and skepticism about analog implementations and energy efficiency claims. The team positions Un-0 as a first step, acknowledging that conventional methods took years of refinement to reach their current state.

On ImageNet 64×64 it reaches FID 6.74, matching the quality of leading conventional image generation methods when they were first published.

Key Highlights
  • Un-0 uses coupled Kuramoto oscillators as the core computational primitive, learning a coupling matrix and frequencies.
  • Achieves FID 6.74 on ImageNet 64×64, competitive with early diffusion and GAN models.
  • Ablations show that the dynamics are performing non-trivial computation beyond random feature extraction.
  • Coupled oscillator systems scale as O(N²), raising concerns for high-resolution image generation.
  • The work is a foundational step toward energy-efficient AI on physical substrates, with open-source release.

An entire Herculaneum scroll has been read for the first time
⬆ 1324 pts 💬 280 HN comments

The Vesuvius Challenge has achieved a historic milestone: the complete virtual unwrapping and reading of PHerc. 1667, a Herculaneum scroll sealed since 79 AD. Using high-resolution phase-contrast X-ray microtomography at the ESRF synchrotron, the team reconstructed the rolled papyrus geometry, flattened it digitally, and applied machine learning models to detect ink traces indistinguishable from carbonized papyrus. This marks the first time a rolled Herculaneum scroll has been read entirely end-to-end without physical opening. The work builds on open science principles—all data, code, and transcriptions are publicly released—and the research team itself emerged from the global contestant community of the Vesuvius Challenge. The recovered text is a Stoic philosophical treatise on ethics, attributed to the circle of Chrysippus, with fragments discussing human nature, impulse, and moral progress.

The breakthrough extends beyond PHerc. 1667. In PHerc. Paris 4 (Scroll 1), higher-resolution imaging made ink directly visible in 3D X-ray data, independently confirming the 2023 Grand Prize readings. In PHerc. 139, the title and author were recovered, identifying it as Philodemus' *On Gods*, Book 8. These results demonstrate a scalable method for reading the hundreds of remaining sealed scrolls. The project's open approach—releasing data at scrollprize.org/data and code on GitHub—enables global verification and rapid progress. A new $1M Grand Prize has been announced to accelerate the effort. The full reading of PHerc. 1667 yields approximately 22 columns of continuous Greek text, including the first clear view of passages like "we will inquire into something, but we will not grasp it, if in some way we depart from ourselves and from our own nature."

The first complete reading of a Herculaneum scroll without ever opening it, using ML to detect invisible ink on carbonized papyrus.

Key Highlights
  • First complete virtual unwrapping and reading of a Herculaneum scroll (PHerc. 1667).
  • Used synchrotron X-ray microtomography and machine learning for ink detection.
  • Recovered ~22 columns of Greek Stoic philosophical text from the 2nd century BC.
  • Open science: all data, code, and transcriptions publicly released.
  • Scalable method promises to unlock hundreds more scrolls from the library.

A notable highlight is the project's timing, coinciding with Google's proposed Open Knowledge Format, which OpenKnowledge claims compatibility with, despite a name collision.

OpenKnowledge combines full WYSIWYG markdown editing with collaborative AI via MCP and CRDT, aiming to be the Obsidian replacement that work with agents as easily as with humans.

IBM debuts sub-1 nanometer chip technology
⬆ 324 pts 💬 175 HN comments

IBM has announced a groundbreaking sub-1 nanometer chip technology, featuring a novel 3D 'nanostack' transistor architecture at the 0.7 nm (7 angstrom) node. This design vertically stacks and staggers transistors using sequential integration, achieving nearly 100 billion transistors on a fingernail-sized chip—double the density of IBM's 2 nm node from 2021. The nanostack architecture employs dual-channel engineering and ultra-thin dielectric bonding, enabling independent optimization of performance and power for each transistor layer. While node names no longer correspond to physical dimensions (actual gate lengths remain above 10 nm), the density and performance gains are significant, with projections of 50% higher performance or 70% better energy efficiency compared to 2 nm. This marks a critical step beyond traditional scaling limits and into the angstrom era.

IBM claims the technology has been validated through functional CMOS inverters and SRAM demonstrations, with a 40% scaling improvement in SRAM density for AI workloads. The company projects production readiness within five years, leveraging a High NA EUV lithography tool from ASML at their Albany research facility. IBM's business model focuses on licensing; recent partners include Rapidus for 2 nm. Jay Gambetta, Director of IBM Research, stated: 'We're not just making smaller transistors, we're reinventing how chips are built.'
