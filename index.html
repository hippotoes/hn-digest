Google open-sources TorchTPU, a native PyTorch back end for TPUs
⬆ 364 pts 💬 76 HN comments

Google has open-sourced TorchTPU (github.com/google-pytorch/torch_tpu), a first-party PyTorch backend targeting Tensor Processing Units under Apache 2.0. Architecturally it is not a thin shim: the repository ships custom ATen kernel implementations for TPU, a compilation cache for memoizing XLA computations, support for data- and tensor-parallel distributed training, integration with torch.compile() for graph-mode execution, and debug/benchmark utilities. Example workloads span Llama, Qwen, ResNet, DLRM, minGPT and UNet, with Marimo notebooks (a Jupyter alternative) runnable from a v6e TPU VM. Installation is pip-based at 0.2.0 via PyTorch's CPU extra-index, with Bazel-based source builds gated by a wheel_common config that fuses the XLA/MLIR backend into a single libxla_base.so. Python 3.11+ only, matching PyTorch's compatibility matrix.

The 4,471 commits, 514 stars and 52 open issues at announcement time indicate a long-running internal project surfaced suddenly rather than a weekend hack — a detail the commentariat mostly missed while debating timing. Strategically, this collapses the historic friction that pushed TPU-curious teams toward JAX or forced them to fight XLA directly, and it lands the same week Amazon ships torch-neuronx for Trainium, coinciding with the PyTorch conference. Commenters immediately connected the dots beyond Google: one has built libtt, an open-source libtpu-compatible layer for Tenstorrent accelerators that already runs most of TorchTPU, and another is driving PJRT from Lean via lean4-mlir. The practical upshot is that the PyTorch/XLA path is becoming the substrate for non-Nvidia, tile-based accelerators generally.

With 4,471 commits and a 0.2.0 PyPI release already on the index, TorchTPU reads as a long-incubated internal project finally surfaced — not the reactive counter-punch some commenters jokingly assumed it to be.

Key Highlights
  • TorchTPU provides native ATen kernels for TPUs plus an XLA compilation cache, not just a PJRT passthrough
  • Distributed training (data and tensor parallelism) and torch.compile() graph-mode integration are first-class
  • Announcement coincided with Amazon's torch-neuronx for Trainium and the PyTorch conference
  • Third parties are already porting the stack: libtt for Tenstorrent, lean4-mlir driving PJRT
  • TPU access remains opaque — rented on GCP, sold on-prem to select customers, or embedded in Coral/Pixel silicon

#4
Voxtral 3
⬆ 724 pts 💬 177 HN comments

Mistral's Voxtral 3 is less a single model drop than a vertically integrated voice stack: a streaming speech-to-text family (Voxtral Small Transcribe 3 for accuracy, a 4B Mini variant for throughput, plus a Realtime Mini model tuned for sub-500ms latency in 35+ languages), a text-to-speech model (Voxtral Mini TTS 3, 35+ languages and 50+ dialects), and a voice agent SDK that fuses listening, reasoning, tool calling, and speaking into one real-time pipeline. The engineering claims worth noting are the unglamorous ones: native diarization, end-of-turn prediction that fuses acoustic and semantic signals to cut both latency and false cut-offs during interruptions, context biasing via keyphrases to lift domain vocabulary recall without fine-tuning, and contextual TTS where the model infers emotion, pacing, and emphasis from the text itself rather than requiring explicit prosody tags. That combination—turn detection plus biasing plus tool-call orchestration—is where most homegrown voice agents actually fail.

The published numbers are aggressively competitive: a 4.1% FLEURS macro-average WER for Voxtral Small Transcribe 3 against Whisper large-v3 at 16.0%, a 18.4% cpWER on diarization versus 38.4% for Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, keyphrase recall jumping from 81.4% to 94.6% with context, and TTS at $13 per 1M characters with a Pro Voice adaptation mode that clones a speaker's character and emotional range from 30 minutes of audio. Mistral is positioning sovereign deployment—EU-hosted API or on-prem—as the differentiator for regulated contact-center workloads. The strategic subtext is unmistakable: this is a closed-weight release, a break from the Voxtral Mini 3B lineage that commenters were running offline, and the community read it instantly as Mistral trading open-ecosystem goodwill for enterprise margin in the one modality where open competition is thinnest.

Pro Voice reproduces a speaker's emotional range and style across languages from just 30 minutes of audio—and Mistral shipped none of Voxtral 3 as open weights, ending the lineage that made Voxtral Mini 3B a favorite for offline inference.

Key Highlights
  • Voxtral 3 bundles STT (Small/Mini/Realtime), TTS (Mini, 35+ languages, 50+ dialects), and an agent SDK into one real-time streaming pipeline with sub-500ms latency.
  • Reported SOTA results: 4.1% FLEURS macro WER vs 16.0% for Whisper large-v3, 18.4% cpWER diarization vs 38.4% for Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, and 94.6% keyphrase recall with context biasing vs 81.4% without.
  • Pricing is aggressive—$0.0035/min for the 4B transcribe model and $13/1M characters for TTS—positioned explicitly against OpenAI TTS-1, ElevenLabs Flash, Deepgram, and Gemini Flash.
  • The release is closed-weight, a departure from Voxtral Mini 3B, which commenters note they run offline on commodity hardware (one shipped commercial Windows software requiring only 4GB VRAM on a Ryzen 5 5600U iGPU).
  • Sovereign deployment (EU-hosted API or self-hosted) is the commercial wedge for contact centers and regulated European enterprises, though critics note ZDR is request-gated and the EU language coverage is incomplete.

Upcoming critical PHP security release (CVE-2026-109618 / -109619)
⬆ 1283 pts 💬 533 HN comments

A critical, unpatched remote-code-execution flaw in PHP — tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 and already nicknamed 'PHP2Shell' — has inverted the normal coordinated-disclosure playbook. Rather than a patch landing first, a leaked demonstration video and privately circulated proof-of-concept forced the PHP release team to pre-announce a fix slated for 14:00 UTC, with binaries for 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35 pending. The bug reportedly spans every supported branch of the interpreter released in the past decade, and commenters describe the primitive as reachable through multipart upload handling and PHP's session upload-progress serialization path — a component almost universally enabled by default. Cloudflare's CTO confirmed 'early attacks emerging' and the WAF vendor is iterating mitigations as new vectors appear; Bunny CDN added rules too. The official advisory host, news-web.php.net, was itself hugged offline, leaving operators to reconstruct guidance from Alpine's GitLab, mailing-list release-note filenames, and third-party summaries.

The operational fallout is unusually public. DigiD (Dutch government login), RTL, the Dutch parliament, most GovCMS-hosted Australian government sites, and large parts of the University of Vienna's estate went dark pre-emptively, while individual consultants reported taking down ten or hundreds of customer applications rather than accept exposure. Widely circulated stopgaps — session.upload_progress.enabled = Off, file_uploads = Off, blocking multipart bodies at the edge, or restricting sites to GET/HEAD — are explicitly described by PHP release managers as insufficient: 'INI settings alone won't protect you.' Cloudflare protection only helps if origin servers actually reject non-Cloudflare traffic, and fcgi versus mod_php topologies change whether killing a process is enough. Early patched builds appeared on Chainguard's image directory before the official window. The thread is also thick with epistemic fog: a claim of full database dumps via a personal site was disputed as non-RCE, and comparisons to Heartbleed were met with scepticism — yet the asymmetry is real, because hundreds of thousands of shared-host and WordPress fleets rarely patch within hours, let alone minutes.

Given that the exploit instructions are circulating at the top of Twitter, it doesn't seem outrageous to assume every major PHP website is already compromised.

A PHP release manager's admission captures the collapse of coordinated disclosure: 'They are still asleep… I am also shocked that the exploit was published that quickly, after the leaked video… We expected a bit more time, even with LLMs.'

Key Highlights
  • Zero-day RCE in PHP (CVE-2026-109618 / -109619) affects essentially every supported release from 8.2 through 8.5, with the fix withheld until 14:00 UTC.
  • Active exploitation is confirmed by Cloudflare and a leaked demo video; PoCs circulate privately, and third-party patched builds appeared before the official release.
  • Suggested mitigations (disable session.upload_progress, file_uploads, block multipart at the edge, GET/HEAD-only maintenance mode) are treated as partial at best by PHP core maintainers.
  • Real-world impact: DigiD, RTL, Dutch parliament, GovCMS-hosted Australian government sites and university services were taken offline pre-emptively.
  • Long-tail exposure is the real risk — shared hosting, unmaintained LAMP/WordPress installs, and over-privileged database roles make mass compromise likely regardless of patch speed.

Feds suspect ransom negotiator ran ShinyHunters extortion site
⬆ 231 pts 💬 111 HN comments

KrebsOnSecurity reports that U.S. federal investigators have identified executives at two ransomware negotiation firms who maintained unusually close ties to ShinyHunters, the crew that breached fbijobs.gov and made off with medical, psychological, and personnel records on FBI employees. One U.S. executive at a consulting firm with more than 1,000 associates is suspected of helping operate Shinywire, the group's victim-facing extortion portal, which was live from roughly May 3 to October 16, 2026 and carried entries for nearly 100 victim organizations, complete with stolen-file samples and seven-day deadlines before publication on the Tor shaming blog. The thread that unravelled the network was operational: Dutch police arrested ShinyHunters suspect Pepijn van der Stap on September 15 while he was actively using Signal, giving investigators his recent message history — and, per sources, revealing that Canadian executive Edward Dubrovsky knew of the FBI data theft before the bureau did.

The structural story is incentives. ShinyHunters has extorted over $70 million in 2026 alone, and the negotiator economy around it runs on contingency fees — CyberAcuView's Mark Camillo notes that firms specializing in ransom negotiation "tend to charge a percentage of the total ransom amount paid," an arrangement he calls an "inherent conflict of interest." DOJ has begun treating that conflict as criminal: Zohar Pinhasi of MonsterCloud was charged in October with paying hackers $8 million while billing clients $19 million, and Angelo Martino drew 70 months for feeding BlackCat his clients' negotiating positions. The PowerSchool case (62 million students, 9.5 million teachers, a reported $2.85 million payment facilitated by CyberSteward) shows the second-order failure: paying did not stop later per-district extortion using the same files. Meanwhile Coveware and Unit 221B, which advise against paying, report harassment from the group for suppressing its payment rate.

Firms that "specialize in helping companies negotiate a ransom payment tend to charge a percentage of the total ransom amount paid" — an inherent conflict of interest, per CyberAcuView CEO Mark Camillo, who says negotiators are financially incentivized never to negotiate the price down.

ShinyHunters extorted more than $70 million in 2026 — while at least one suspected negotiator allegedly helped run the very portal pressing victims to pay.

Key Highlights
  • FBI suspects executives at two negotiation firms of unusually close ties to ShinyHunters; at least one allegedly helped operate the Shinywire extortion site (~100 victim entries, live May–October 2026).
  • Van der Stap's arrest while logged into Signal gave Dutch and U.S. investigators his message history, exposing contacts with negotiators who knew of breaches before victims did.
  • Percentage-of-ransom fee structures create a documented conflict of interest, now being tested in court via the Pinhasi and Martino prosecutions.
  • PowerSchool's reported $2.85M payment did not prevent a second wave of extortion against individual school districts using the same stolen files.
  • Incident-response firms advising against payment (Coveware, Unit 221B) were targeted with harassment by ShinyHunters for suppressing its conversion rate.

#3
Show HN: SimCity, but with Permits
⬆ 849 pts 💬 391 HN comments

Permit Pending is a browser-based city builder whose inversion is its thesis: you are not a god with a bulldozer, you are an elected mayor with a zoning board. Built by Peter Cai on Three.js and React 18 with Vite, it ships as a ~1MB bundle (290KB gzipped) with no router and no Redux/Zustand, custom-built charts, and IndexedDB plus a service worker so saves stay local and the game runs offline after the first visit. Under the UI sits an actual agent economy — residents carry ages, incomes and homes; businesses hire, grow and leave; land values, rents, jobs and taxes interlock — while a seven-seat council, public hearings, appeals and four-year elections gate every apartment block and bus lane. Scenarios scale from a 200-resident crossroads hamlet to a 300,000-resident 'Unaffordable Metropolis' with entrenched incumbents.

The narrative engine is where the seams show, and where the HN thread became most technically interesting. Commenters caught simulation leakage — goda90 reported petitions 'with signatures from >250% of the town population' and traffic complaints at a '4-way intersection in front of the feed store' that did not exist — revealing prewritten story arcs retrofitted onto procedural city state rather than fully templated from it; Cai acknowledged the corpus debts and said he'd gone 'back to retrofit them into the engine.' Others hit hard failures: zamadatix logged 'glRenderbufferStorageMultisample: Texture total allocation size is too large' on a black screen, which Cai fixed by having Claude 'fall back to lower resolution textures.' The game's designed loop — a 12-story rezoning filed in Month 3, chopped to 6 stories by Month 7 for parking, dead by Month 8, appealed to Month 11, still unresolved at Month 42 — is the article's real artifact: a playable argument that the binding constraint on housing is procedural, not economic.

"I support new housing. Just not here." — Concerned Neighbor, Northgate. The game's canonical timeline says it all: a 12-story building filed in Month 3 is 6 stories by Month 7, unprofitable by Month 8, under appeal through Month 42, and still blamed for high rents on election day.

Key Highlights
  • Deliberate anti-SimCity design: every improvement generates an opposition group, so the simulation models political cost rather than optimizing away scarcity
  • Narrative text is largely prewritten story arcs retrofitted onto procedural city state, causing immersion-breaking leaks (petition signatures exceeding population, phantom intersections)
  • Technical stack independently reverse-engineered by commenters: Three.js, React 18 + Vite, custom charts, no router or global store, IndexedDB persistence with service worker offline support, 290KB gzipped
  • Multiple hard failures reported — WebGL framebuffer allocation overruns and a 'PP-SIM-F99D' simulation start crash — with the author patching in lower-resolution texture fallback via an LLM-assisted fix
  • The comment thread metastasized into land-use policy debate: ministerial vs. discretionary approval, zoning as exclusionary-by-design, Japan's rules-based national system vs. American local control, and the unrepresented interests of future residents

What shall I teach my son?
Steve Krouse, a working programmer expecting his first child, uses Neil Postman's 1995 book The End of Education as a lens to ask what education is *for* once the economically valuable skill he intends to transmit — software engineering — no longer reliably converts into a livelihood. His argument is structural rather than sentimental: education has never had one purpose, but a shifting table of purposes (moral formation, citizenship, employment, world-making), and institutions persist not because their stated justification holds but because of unspoken perpetuating forces — endowments, prestige, credentialing, and the sheer utility of supervised childcare. He invokes Taleb's Lindy effect to explain why Harvard survived the death of its clerical mission, and why technologists who predict schools' imminent demise (Krouse's own 2013 MOOC prediction included) have a perfect track record of being wrong. The essay's sharpest move is to notice that his own preferred telos — 'resourceful,' the capacity to wield arbitrary resources to uncover and solve problems — has just been automated out of existence by the agentic tool loop. Having lost the market as an objective arbiter of educational...

The Other Epidemic: Trying to get off benzodiazepines
The Believer article frames benzodiazepine dependence as a largely iatrogenic epidemic: patients are prescribed GABA-A positive allosteric modulators for anxiety, insomnia, and muscle spasm, then discover that chronic use produces tolerance and neuroadaptation that make discontinuation unusually difficult. The HN thread turns the abstract policy issue into lived testimony. Commenters describe rebound anxiety, insomnia, sensory hypersensitivity, palpitations, and seizures, and repeatedly stress that benzodiazepine withdrawal is not opioid withdrawal with a different logo: it is often longer, weirder, and potentially fatal. The clinical difficulty is heterogeneity—risk is not cleanly predicted by dose or duration, so some people taper easily while others suffer for months after short, low-dose exposure. Specific data in the comments sharpen the scale: one quoted estimate says 30.6 million American adults used benzodiazepines in the prior year, 25.3 million as prescribed. Withdrawal hellscape Survivors and witnesses describe benzodiazepine withdrawal as qualitatively worse than opioid withdrawal: “order of magnitude more intense,” with a timeline of “weeks/months rather than days.” One reports five ER trips, three hospitalizations, and a full year before relief; another describes “crying like a bitch... every single morning” and skin that goes “on fire.” They frame benzos as uniquely dangerous because dependence can be iatrogenic and protracted, and because low-dose or short-term exposure sometimes suffices. This cohort supplies the thread’s emotional core and pushes for much stronger warnings. (~75 users)
Prescriber negligence This cluster focuses on informed consent and medical-system failure. Commenters report a psychiatrist who “told me nothing,” lorazepam framed as “just like having a small cold,” doctors who “casually recommend benzos,” and symptoms dismissed as “all in my head” once mental-health history is seen. They argue clinicians underweight dependence risk because benzodiazepines have a wide overdose ceiling compared with barbiturates and therefore seem “safe.” The demanded remedy is not necessarily prohibition but accountability, truthful risk disclosure, and far more caution in primary and psychiatric prescribing. (~55 users)
Medical utility defense A counterweight insists benzodiazepines retain real value. One user has taken bromazepam occasionally for 25 years with no addiction; another says a 10-pill Xanax prescription lasts three months for flying. Others argue that stable long-term patients should be “left alone,” that surgical and procedural medicine has “no replacement” for benzos, and that “banned, period” overgeneralizes from terrible cases. They emphasize individual variation and caution against moral panic, while usually conceding that down-sides and dependence risk need wider awareness. (~50 users)
Taper pragmatics This group accepts the severity but concentrates on recovery mechanics and anxiety treatment. Comments cite the Ashton taper protocol, careful Valium cross-tapers, “dividing the tiny tablets into 8ths,” fixed-time dosing to avoid reinforcing maladaptive PRN conditioning, and radical exposure to anxiety rather than responding to the “false fire alarm.” Others mention promethazine as a sleep aid or note that two-week Valium use caused no issues. The tone is practical: protocols matter, cross-tapers need management, and non-drug deconditioning is the long-term exit. (~35 users)
