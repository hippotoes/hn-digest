Claude solves two open problems in the physics of black holes
⬆ 527 pts 💬 254 HN comments

Anthropic's science team reports that Claude Fable 5.2 resolved two genuine open problems in quantum gravity, both artifacts of the holographic duality program. The first completes a three-loop perturbative calculation in N=4 super Yang-Mills theory that Aharony, Marsano, Minwalla, Papadodimas and Van Raamsdonk outlined in 2003 and never finished: extracting a finite number from thousands of Feynman diagrams (with the usual infinity-taming subtleties) to determine whether the weak-coupling deconfinement transition is abrupt like the Hawking-Page transition or smooth. It is abrupt, mirroring the gravity-side result and suggesting the gas-to-black-hole switch is intrinsic to the quantum theory rather than an artifact of general relativity. The second attacks the modular bootstrap in 3D gravity/2D CFT, where Claude spotted a pattern in numerical data that physicists had missed, imported a method from high-dimensional sphere packing, and proved the exact upper bound on the lightest black hole mass — showing the ceiling cannot be pushed lower.

The methodology is arguably the more consequential disclosure: researcher Hongbin Chen asked Claude to enumerate 22 interesting open problems; autonomous agents then ran unsupervised for roughly six weeks, cross-checking each other, with most of the Hawking-Page calculation done twice by independent copies. A separate human physicist using Claude reached the same answer weeks later by a different route, providing mutual validation; one half of the bootstrap proof follows the strategy of a sphere-packing proof recently produced by an OpenAI model; results were formalized in Lean before the paper was written with minimal guidance. No human is credited as author — a first that drew the sharpest criticism. With 527 points and 254 comments, the thread is dominated less by the physics than by disputes over attribution, whether 'open problem' means what readers think, and Anthropic's timing relative to its IPO.

#2
Show HN: Fast deterministic natural language search for OpenStreetMap data
⬆ 95 pts 💬 25 HN comments

HippoMaps' Show HN presents deterministic natural-language search over OpenStreetMap data. Rather than relying on an LLM to infer semantics at query time, it translates plain-language requests into structured spatial and OSM-tag filters—feature type, proximity constraints, named geography—and executes them against a prebuilt index. The example query, cafes by the Limmat 100 m from the lake in Zurich, illustrates the core technical challenge: natural language is a friendlier interface to a query compiler, not a replacement for geospatial reasoning. This matters because ordinary map apps expose brittle filter UIs, while deterministic execution offers speed, reproducibility, and explainability. The project is GPL 3.0, and the author says LLMs helped with coding, not runtime search.

Community comments add concrete implementation and cost data. The author says HippoMaps runs on a 2019 4-core AMD machine with 64 GB RAM and 1 TB SSD, with map tiles on a separate, beefier server; selling Overpass access roughly covers costs. Full offline use is not feasible because tiles, query engine, routing, navigation, and languages need about 3 TB of SSD. Users praise speed and feature mappings but expose ontology gaps: a dispensary query mapped to pharmacies and returned a CVS, while houses away from sewage works and loud pubs are not yet supported—though pubs and train stations already work. Requests for CoMaps funding and offline support show that OSM search is data-intensive infrastructure, not just UI polish.

Natural-language determinism trades semantic coverage for predictability: every miss becomes an explicit taxonomy or curation problem rather than a model hallucination.

#3
Floppy3.5: A portable 3.5-inch floppy WiFi NAS
⬆ 84 pts 💬 30 HN comments

floppy3.5 is a fully open, self-contained hardware project that turns a slimline 3.5" floppy drive into a battery-powered WiFi NAS — an answer to a problem most of us forgot we had: extracting JPEGs from Sony Mavica-style cameras that wrote to 1.44 MB FAT12 media. The architecture is the interesting part. A single custom PCB hosts two microcontrollers in a producer/consumer split: an RP2354A acts as the flux-level controller, deliberately speaking Keir Fraser's Greaseweazle host protocol over USB CDC (VID:PID 2E8A:000A), so the existing `gw read --format ibm.1440` toolchain works unmodified. Its flux engine was reworked away from Greaseweazle's STM32 timers onto the RP2350's PIO programs and DMA. An ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8 then bridges over a custom SPI frame format defined in a shared header, decodes the FAT12 image, does SHA-256 content comparison to deduplicate copies into /DISKnnnn/ folders on microSD, and serves a web UI via mDNS at floppy.local, with OTA updates for both chips.

The supporting engineering is unusually complete for a hobby release: BQ25180 charger plus MAX17048 fuel gauge, LTC2954 power-button controller, TPS63020/TPS61022 rails (3.3 V logic, 5 V drive), RV-3028-C7 RTC, a 1.9" ST7789 170×320 LCD rendered as a faux segment display with 'ghost' inactive segments, and a track map showing 80 cylinders × 2 heads where half-lit blocks denote retry success and blinking blocks denote bad sectors. Everything — Gerbers, schematic, assembly drawing, STL/3MF housing, both firmwares, and host-runnable unit tests using TinyCC/gcc/clang — ships under the Unlicense. The candid 'Quirks' section is the most instructive part: the BQ25180's default 1000 mA over-current limit trips when the spindle spins up, so firmware must set charger limits at every boot, and a simultaneous read plus full-power WiFi TX can approach the 1500 mA battery limit.

The controller is a functional Greaseweazle port to the RP2354 — PIO programs and DMA replace the STM32 timers of the original flux engine — meaning a pocket device with a floppy slot still answers to `gw read --device <PORT> --format ibm.1440 disk.img`.

Key Highlights
  • Dual-MCU design: RP2354A flux controller speaking the Greaseweazle host protocol, bridged over a custom SPI frame format to an ESP32-S3 that decodes FAT12 and serves the web UI
  • SHA-256 content hashing prevents duplicate photo copies across /DISKnnnn/ folders, working around cameras restarting filenames at MVC-00001 after a format
  • Full-stack open release under the Unlicense: Gerbers, schematic, assembly drawing, 3MF/STL housing, both firmwares (pico-sdk 2.x and ESP-IDF 5.2+), plus host-only unit tests
  • A candid quirks section documents real power-budget hazards: BQ25180 defaults trip on spindle spin-up, and concurrent disk reads plus full-power WiFi TX approach the 1500 mA charger limit
  • The 1.9" ST7789 UI renders a segment-display aesthetic with ghosted inactive icons and a live 80-cylinder track map distinguishing clean, retried, and bad tracks
