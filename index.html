#1
Hy4 preview
⬆ 347 pts 💬 208 HN comments

Tencent has released and open-sourced Hy4 preview, a Mixture-of-Experts LLM with 770B total parameters and 49B active parameters, alongside a context window exceeding 1M tokens. It is positioned as a top-tier open-weight model for real-world productivity, with architectural and training advances in both pre- and post-training. The model is available under Apache 2.0 and through Tencent Cloud TokenHub, OpenRouter, and apps like WorkBuddy, CodeBuddy, Yuanbao, and ima. Tencent reports internal blind evals with 163 experts that place Hy4 preview slightly ahead of GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3 for engineering tasks. The release emphasizes pragmatic productivity: long-context coding, office analytics, game prototyping from natural-language prompts, and scientific research support. Pricing undercuts major rivals, at $0.834 per million input tokens, $2.501 per million output tokens, and $0.042 per million tokens for cache hits.

More striking than raw benchmarks is Hy4 preview's claimed first contribution to its own development. Tencent describes an early-stage recursive self-improvement loop: the model proposed training methods, data strategies, low-level operator optimizations, ran experiments, and fed resulting code and logs back into later iterations. It also autonomously optimized its inference system, improving end-to-end throughput by 31.8% via operator fusion and communication tweaks. This 'model improving its own infrastructure' angle fuels both excitement and unease. The HN community is split: some report that predecessor Hy3 was surprisingly capable in agentic and coding tests, while others dismiss 'open-sourced' as open weights, question chart honesty, and worry about the geopolitical and economic AI race. Users such as simonw even posted detailed reasoning traces comparing model quality, and several report practical tooling issues like rate limits, region blocks, and performance concerns.

Hy4 preview autonomously optimized its own inference system, improving end-to-end throughput by 31.8%—an early example of a model editing the infrastructure it runs on.

Key Highlights
  • 770B total / 49B active MoE with >1M token context, released under Apache 2.0.
  • Priced at $0.834/M input and $2.501/M output; cache hits just $0.042/M.
  • Internal blind eval: 2.99/4.00 across 203 engineering tasks, ahead of GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3.
  • First reported recursive self-improvement loop: Hy4 proposed training and inference optimizations that boosted throughput by 31.8%.
  • Deep product integration with WorkBuddy, CodeBuddy, Yuanbao, ima, and API via Tencent Cloud TokenHub and OpenRouter.

#3
Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope
⬆ 231 pts 💬 85 HN comments

The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope—named after NASA's first chief astronomer—launched successfully on August 30, 2026, aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy from Kennedy Space Center's LC-39A. Now en route to the Sun-Earth L2 halo orbit, Roman carries a 2.4-meter primary mirror originally built for a classified National Reconnaissance Office satellite. Its primary instrument, the Wide Field Instrument, packs 18 infrared detectors into a 288-megapixel focal plane covering 0.28 square degrees—about 100 times Hubble's near-infrared field of view. A Coronagraph Instrument will directly image exoplanets by blocking starlight with deformable masks. Roman is a survey-first complement to JWST: it will map dark energy via weak lensing and baryon acoustic oscillations, complete a statistical exoplanet census via microlensing, and open a wide window on time-domain infrared astrophysics.

The mission's data policy is as radical as its optics: roughly 1.4 TB of raw compressed science data per day—downlinked at about 500 Mbps—will be made public immediately after processing, with no proprietary embargo. HN commenter delichon notes anyone can download it and 'be the first person to see a galaxy.' The reuse of an obsolete spy-satellite primary mirror helped NASA stay under budget and ahead of schedule, though commenters worry about the single-copy architecture and political interference. With Rubin, Hubble, and JWST already online, Roman adds wide-area time-domain discovery to an ecosystem optimized for rapid follow-up, promising to reshape dark-energy constraints, exoplanet demographics, and our view of the transient sky over the next decade.

Roman's field of view is at least 100 times larger than Hubble's, with a 1.4 TB/day open-data pipeline where every observation becomes public with no embargo.

Key Highlights
  • Roman launched Aug 30, 2026 on a SpaceX Falcon Heavy from LC-39A, targeting Sun-Earth L2.
  • Its 2.4-m mirror is a reused National Reconnaissance Office spy-satellite telescope, lowering cost.
  • The Wide Field Instrument has 288 megapixels across 18 detectors and 100x Hubble's near-infrared field of view.
  • Raw downlink is ~1.4 TB/day at 500 Mbps; all processed data are public with no embargo.
  • Roman will survey dark energy, exoplanets, and transient phenomena, complementing JWST and ground-based observatories like Rubin.

#2
Lawmakers added $1 to car insurance policies. That money paid for Flock cameras
⬆ 340 pts 💬 212 HN comments

In 2023, the Texas Legislature unanimously passed a law adding a $1 annual fee to auto insurance policies, ostensibly to fund anti-catalytic-converter-theft programs. Three years later, an investigative report by The Texas Tribune reveals that the Motor Vehicle Crime Prevention Authority (MVCPA), a state agency with a governor-appointed board, redirected at least $30 million of this revenue toward expanding Flock's AI-powered license plate reader network. The fee has generated roughly $81 million, of which $50.8 million financed 234 grants, including at least 3,200 Flock cameras across the state—about 95 grants funding ~2,000 local cameras, plus $15.9 million for 1,183 Texas DPS cameras, and another $3 million recently approved for 583 more. The agency's chair, Laredo Police Chief Miguel Rodriguez, explicitly stated ambitions to cover the entire state with cameras, even using DPS rights-of-way to bypass local communities that have voted to remove Flock.

Flock cameras use AI to create a 'vehicle fingerprint'—tracking license plates, make, model, color, and distinguishing details—all stored in a shared national database accessible to law enforcement without a warrant. The article highlights 62,000 ALPR searches in 2025, which led to 1,660 cleared catalytic converter cases, but also a sharp surge in privacy backlash. Multiple lawmakers who voted for the fee, including the bill's author, Sen. Carol Alvarado, said they never anticipated surveillance cameras; Rep. Brian Harrison called it 'warrantless surveillance' he would not have supported. Governor Greg Abbott responded to the report by pausing state funding for local Flock grants, while citing recent indictments of officers for misusing camera data. Nearly 100 U.S. municipalities have terminated Flock contracts, and Texas has seen grassroots anti-surveillance groups and acts of vandalism. The story underscores the tension between crime-fighting efficacy and the expanding, lightly regulated use of mass surveillance infrastructure.

A $1 auto-insurance fee intended for catalytic converter theft has funded at least 3,200 Flock cameras—roughly one in four of all such cameras in Texas.

Key Highlights
  • The $1/year insurance fee was sold as an anti-catalytic-converter measure but mostly funded Flock license plate readers.
  • MVCPA awarded at least 95 grants for ~2,000 local Flock cameras, plus $15.9M to DPS for 1,183 and $3M more for 583 tollway cameras.
  • Flock's AI builds a vehicle fingerprint searchable nationwide by law enforcement without a warrant.
  • The article reports 62,000 ALPR searches in 2025 leading to 1,660 cleared catalytic converter cases, but privacy misuse cases are mounting.
  • Governor Abbott paused state funds for Flock grants after the investigation, and many municipalities have canceled contracts.
