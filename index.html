The state of open source AI
⬆ 436 pts 💬 315 HN comments

Mozilla's 2026 report on open-source AI declares that open-weight models have achieved near-parity with closed frontier models on many tasks—coding, instruction-following, general knowledge—while the capability gap has narrowed from 8% to about 3.3%, concentrated in reasoning and agentic tasks. The report highlights a stunning collapse in inference costs: GPT-4-class pricing fell 50× in 36 months, from $20 to $0.40 per 1M tokens, making open models economically irresistible. By mid-2026, open weights route the majority of tokens on OpenRouter, and five of the top nine models by volume are open. Yet the open stack suffers a chronic operational gap: only 51% of open-model teams reach production versus 63% for closed, hindered by tooling, security, and compliance challenges. The report argues that the real contest has shifted to the 'agentic harness'—the orchestration layer above models—where closed labs are building proprietary moats.

The strategic case for open models extends beyond cost: they offer sovereignty and exit rights. The article cites examples from New Zealand (Māori speech models), East Africa (cassava disease diagnosis offline), and Switzerland (national model trained on public supercomputers). It warns that closed APIs reproduce cloud-era vendor lock-in, with egress penalties and unilateral shutdowns like Anthropic's Fable 5 incident. Financial data reveals the open ecosystem is now a multibillion-dollar market: Databricks at $5.4B run-rate, Mistral at $400M ARR, DeepSeek raising $7.4B. However, the report itself faces sharp community criticism for its heavily AI-generated prose, which many readers find disingenuous and hard to parse. The underlying data—especially the 50× cost collapse and open-token growth—is widely acknowledged as compelling, even as the presentation detracts from the message.

Inference cost for GPT-4-class models fell 50× in 36 months, from $20 to $0.40 per 1M tokens—faster than dotcom-era bandwidth or PC-compute price curves.

Key Highlights
  • Open-weight models have nearly closed the capability gap with closed models, especially in coding and general knowledge, trailing only in reasoning and agentic tasks.
  • Inference costs have collapsed 50× in three years, with open models now 6× cheaper per call than comparable closed models at 90% parity.
  • Open models now route the majority of tokens on OpenRouter, with Chinese open models dominating volume (3:1 over US-built models by mid-2026).
  • Only 51% of open-model teams reach production vs. 63% for closed, due to operational gaps in tooling, security, and deployment complexity.

Kimi K3, and what we can still learn from the pelican benchmark
⬆ 335 pts 💬 179 HN comments

Moonshot AI’s Kimi K3 enters the frontier model race with 2.8 trillion parameters, making it the largest open-weight model yet, surpassing DeepSeek’s 1.6T. Priced at $3/$15 per million input/output tokens, it matches Anthropic’s Sonnet tier and is the most expensive Chinese AI model to date. Self-reported benchmarks show K3 beating Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 on most metrics, though it trails Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol. Notably, Artificial Analysis reports a 21% reduction in output token usage compared to K2.6, and a 732-point Elo gain on long-horizon knowledge work. The model also leads the Frontend Code arena, surpassing even Fable 5 in that domain.

Simon Willison’s long-running “pelican benchmark”—generating an SVG of a pelican riding a bicycle—remains a quirky but revealing hello-world test. K3’s attempt cost $0.25, consumed 95 input tokens and 16,658 output tokens (13,241 reasoning), and produced a competent SVG. Willison notes the benchmark has lost predictive correlation with model quality; GLM-5.2, for example, outperforms far more capable models like Fable 5 on this task. Yet he defends the exercise as a forcing function to test model access, cost, reasoning effort, and geometric/spatial reasoning. The article underscores that the penultimate challenge for modern models is agentic tool use at scale, which the pelican benchmark simply does not address.

Kimi K3’s pelican cost $0.25 and required 13,241 reasoning tokens for 3,417 response tokens, highlighting the hidden costs of “thinking” effort in frontier models.

While helium alone is inert and insufficient for life as we know it, the presence of an atmosphere suggests the planet may harbor more complex molecules deeper down, potentially including water vapor or biosignature gases. This underscores the need for breakthroughs in propulsion or indirect observation, such as solar lens telescopes. Meanwhile, NASA's exoplanet catalog and upcoming TOLIMAN mission aim to image nearby systems directly. The detection of helium escape provides a lower bound on the planet's gravity and atmospheric retention, offering constraints for future modeling of exoplanet climates and potential biosignatures.

At 48 light-years, LHS 1140b is the first rocky planet in the habitable zone confirmed to have an atmosphere—but that atmosphere is helium, chemically inert and incapable of supporting life.
