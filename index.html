⬆ 137 pts 💬 71 HN comments

The story, based on a paper titled 'Thinking Fast, Slow, and Artificial: How AI Is Reshaping Human Reasoning', explores the concept of AI as a 'System 3' in human cognitive processes, building on Daniel Kahneman's dual-process theory of thinking (System 1 for fast, intuitive thought and System 2 for slow, analytical reasoning). This new system represents AI's role in augmenting or supplanting human decision-making, particularly through large language models (LLMs) and other AI tools that offer rapid information processing. The technical foundation lies in how AI interfaces with human cognition, potentially creating a hybrid reasoning layer that can enhance efficiency but also introduce biases and errors, as AI systems are inherently 'jagged'—excel in some tasks while failing in others—leading to complex trust dynamics and cognitive offloading.

From the Hacker News comments, specific data points emerge: users like vicchenai note that in financial data analysis, reliance on LLMs led to accepting outputs without critical checks, illustrating cognitive autopilot. The paper's findings, inferred from comments, suggest that time pressure and incentives modulate AI's impact—when accurate, it buffers costs and amplifies gains; when faulty, it reduces accuracy. Societally, this reshapes expertise, as highlighted by wazHFsRy's concern that AI-generated content blurs lines between real and perceived expertise, potentially devaluing human skill. Implementation details include the need for empirical validation of AI's cognitive effects, with debates on whether AI improves problem-solving or fosters dependency, as seen in mixed user experiences.

The paper introduces AI as a 'System 3' that can amplify or reduce human accuracy depending on trust and situational factors, with studies showing it buffers time-pressure costs when accurate but consistently reduces accuracy when faulty.

Key Highlights
  • AI is framed as a 'System 3' in human reasoning, extending Kahneman's dual-process theory.
  • Trust in AI correlates with surrender to its outputs, affecting decision-making accuracy.
  • Cognitive offloading to AI can lead to autopilot behavior and atrophy of human reasoning skills.

Ubuntu 26.04 Ends 46 Years of Silent sudo Passwords
⬆ 364 pts 💬 355 HN comments

Ubuntu 26.04 LTS, codenamed Resolute Raccoon and scheduled for April 2026, marks a significant departure from a 46-year Unix tradition by defaulting to asterisk feedback for sudo password prompts, ending the era of silent input. This change is driven by sudo-rs, a Rust-based rewrite of the classic C implementation, introduced in Ubuntu 25.10 to enhance memory safety and modernize core utilities. The silent prompt originated in 1980 with sudo's creation for shared terminal environments, where hiding password length protected against shoulder surfing. The transition reflects a broader industry shift towards prioritizing user experience in command-line interfaces, leveraging Rust's safety guarantees to reduce vulnerabilities while addressing long-standing UX pain points, such as user confusion during password entry over high-latency connections or with faulty hardware.

The article details intense debate around the security implications, with developers arguing that exposing password length via asterisks offers negligible risk compared to UX benefits, as graphical login screens already provide feedback. Users can revert to silent prompts by editing sudoers with 'Defaults !pwfeedback'. Community reactions, exemplified by Hacker News comments, show widespread support for the change, citing reduced onboarding friction for newcomers, while dissenters emphasize potential remote vulnerabilities in livestreams. This move is part of Ubuntu's larger modernization effort, including GNOME 50 on Wayland and Linux kernel 7.0, signaling a balance between tradition and accessibility that could influence other distributions and core utilities, potentially lowering barriers to Linux adoption while maintaining configurability for experts.

Starting with the upcoming LTS release, every keystroke at a sudo password prompt will echo an asterisk — a small UX fix that has ignited one of Linux's fiercest debates in years.

Key Highlights
  • Ubuntu 26.04 changes default sudo password feedback from silent to asterisks, breaking a 46-year tradition.
  • Enabled by sudo-rs, a Rust rewrite for enhanced memory safety and modern feature integration.
  • Security debate centers on exposing password length versus improving user experience and reducing confusion.
  • Revertible via sudoers configuration with 'Defaults !pwfeedback', preserving user control.
  • Part of broader Ubuntu modernization, including Rust-based utilities and updated desktop environments.
