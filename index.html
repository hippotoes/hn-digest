OpenAI Jalapeño: Better than Nvidia Blackwell
⬆ 466 pts 💬 301 HN comments

OpenAI's 'Jalapeño' inference ASIC, co-developed with Broadcom and unveiled at Hot Chips, is positioned as a general-purpose LLM inference engine rather than a model-specific accelerator. Built on TSMC N3P with HBM4, a 700W reticle-sized die delivering 13.4 PFLOPs of MXFP4 (B0 stepping), and 15.4TB/s memory bandwidth, it reportedly beats Nvidia's Blackwell—and even Vera Rubin using MTP—on tokens per MW in SemiAnalysis' InferenceX benchmarks, all with single-token prediction and no disaggregation. The architecture uses an out-of-order core with L1 caches, HBM slices with a dedicated collective network, and a minimal memory hierarchy to avoid fixed latencies. Design went from staff to tape-out in ~16 months, aided by AI-assisted chip design and Codex-generated kernels, signalling a potential shift from CUDA's software moat to hardware/software co-design.

SemiAnalysis' tests used 8k1k workloads and OpenAI-provided numbers, with in-person verification but no AgentX results; comparisons to Blackwell are acknowledged as incomplete since Jalapeño targets Rubin-class HBM4 systems. Production ramps in 2027, so near-term impact is limited, but the implication is significant: power-limited datacenters maximize revenue per MW, and Jalapeño reportedly achieves higher output-token throughput per MW than Rubin's published figures, even though Rubin uses speculative decoding. OpenAI's homogenous pool—no prefill/decode separation—trades peak efficiency for adaptability across reasoning/agentic workloads. The article argues that frontier-lab ASICs can now out-iterate merchant silicon, and comments react with doubt (SemiAnalysis' credibility), historical skepticism about custom hardware, and economic speculation about token commodification, IPO timing, and Nvidia's margins. If validated, this could accelerate the disaggregation of AI hardware from GPU-centric procurement.

Jalapeño's A0 engineering sample—just nine months after tape-out—delivered higher tokens/MW than Vera Rubin's published MTP figures, while using only single-token prediction and no speculative decoding.

Key Highlights
  • Jalapeño is a generalized inference ASIC, not OpenAI-model-specific; performs well on third-party models like DeepSeek R1 and Kimi K2.5.
  • Perf/W leadership: beats Blackwell/Rubin on tokens/MW in SemiAnalysis' tests, even without speculative decoding or prefill/decode disaggregation.
  • Architecture: OoO cores, L1 cache, HBM slices, dedicated collective network, minimal memory hierarchy to cut fixed latencies; AI-assisted design.
  • Fast bring-up: 16 months from team to tape-out; Codex used for kernel generation/optimization; B0 stepping in fab with ~25% perf/W gain.
  • Caveats: benchmarks were 8k1k, OpenAI-provided numbers, no AgentX; production volume only in 2027, giving Nvidia/Rubin time to respond.

Bomb fishing is wreaking havoc on Indonesia's coral reefs
⬆ 313 pts 💬 162 HN comments

A new acoustic study reveals that blast fishing in Indonesia's Spermonde Archipelago is far more pervasive than previously documented, with an estimated 8,500 explosions per year destroying critical coral reef habitat in the Coral Triangle. Marine scientists from the Zoological Society of London deployed underwater microphones for 16 months and used open-source AI models to sift through the audio, identifying 3,500 likely bomb detonations in just 3,600 hours of recordings. Each blast stuns and kills fish within a 90-foot radius and turns roughly 200 square feet of living reef into lifeless rubble. The practice is illegal but persists because enforcement is sparse across Indonesia's vast marine protected areas. Unlike climate-driven bleaching, bomb fishing is an acute stressor—if the bombing stops, restoration can succeed.

The research offers a technical path forward: the AI detector, now on GitHub, can distinguish bomb detonations from boat-engine misfires with manual verification, and sound's high speed in water lets it detect blasts from over 10 miles away. Data show detonations occur year-round, peak in the morning, and drop sharply on Fridays, the local day of prayer. The study challenges the simple poverty narrative by noting that costly boats and explosives make this more common among middle-income fishers. 'Repeated blasts create shifting fields of rubble that prevent hard coral recruits from settling,' said James Cook University's Melissa Hampton-Smith. A GPS-synced sensor network could give authorities real-time localization, and the open-source code is meant for global deployment.

A single bomb can wreck 200 square feet of reef, and off Sulawesi a blast detonates once every 62 minutes — 'These picturesque reefs get converted into a landscape that looks like the moon.'

Key Highlights
  • AI-enabled acoustic monitoring in the Spermonde Archipelago captured 3,500 explosions in 3,600 hours, suggesting 8,500+ blasts per year.
  • Each blast has a 90-foot kill radius and destroys ~200 sq ft of coral reef; the damaged area is equivalent to three football fields annually.
  • Blast fishing occurs year-round, peaks in the morning, and dips dramatically on Fridays (prayer day), indicating a social and religious rhythm.
  • The economic analysis suggests middle-income fishers, not the poorest, are often behind bomb fishing due to high startup costs for boats and explosives.
  • The team's open-source AI detector is intended to power a GPS-synced acoustic sensor network for real-time blast localization and enforcement, with global applicability.

Show HN: I made a Raspberry with Qwen my local car AI
⬆ 135 pts 💬 38 HN comments

CarWatch is an ambitious open-source project that turns a Raspberry Pi 5 into a fully offline in-car AI assistant. At its core is a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B model (14.3 GB quantized dynamic) running via llama.cpp at 3.5 tok/s on a ~€300 Pi 5 with 16 GB RAM. The system integrates a continuously listening voice interface (energy VAD + whisper.cpp), a retrieval-augmented generator over the car's 745-page owner's manual with page citations, and live self-knowledge of CPU temperature, throttling, memory, and network state. All code is Python standard library — no pip dependencies — and everything runs as systemd services that self-start, auto-update from GitHub, and maintain reachability through a dial-out tunnel. The project's stated philosophy is 'local is the product; online is the enrichment,' with an explicit honesty policy that refuses to answer anything not grounded in the manual or live sensor data.

The technical stack is impressive but the community remains split. One commenter warns that 'local models and frontier models are very bad at the specifics when it comes to cars,' citing failures like identifying the engine in a 1994 Nissan Skyline. Another questions the offline claim: 'What's the point in having it use an offline LLM if it still requires an internet connection to send all your chat messages through the Groupmind paid service?' Others challenge the project's readiness, noting README admissions like 'Unverified against the real car — it will confirm or refute itself on the next drive.' The project has already gathered 162 stars and is positioned as a vendor-neutral alternative to manufacturer cloud lock-in. The M6 is Apple's first 2-nanometer chip, featuring a 12-core CPU (2 super, 4 performance, 6 efficiency cores), a 12-core GPU with Neural Accelerators, and a Dual 16-core Neural Engine providing up to 2x the peak compute of previous generations. With up to 170GB/s of unified memory bandwidth and 32GB of RAM, it's designed for everyday users, developers, and AI hobbyists. The M5 Ultra, using next-generation UltraFusion, connects two dual-die M5 Max chips into a quad-die architecture—a first for Apple silicon. It offers up to a 36-core CPU, 80-core GPU, 32-core Neural Engine, and a staggering 1.2TB/s of unified memory bandwidth with 512GB of memory, enabling huge LLMs with hundreds of billions of parameters to run entirely on device. Both chips are positioned as performance-per-watt leaders, with Apple touting 'the world's fastest CPU core.'

Some commenters claim Apple's hardware is unmatched, but the macOS software ecosystem remains a sticking point, with users longing for Linux and Vulkan support. Rumors that Apple may skip M6 Pro/Max/Ultra to accelerate an AI-focused M7 add further uncertainty. Overall, the community is split: impressed by the raw specs and 2nm process, yet skeptical about premium pricing, memory upgrade costs, and whether on-device AI truly outpaces cloud inference. The release signals Apple's bet on bringing AI workloads locally, potentially reshaping developer workflows.

The fix, committed to CPython, adds a small exception table so the IDNA codec behaves exactly as Unicode 3.2.0 for the relevant mappings. The vulnerability is tracked as CVE-2026-17084.
