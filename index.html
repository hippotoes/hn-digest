His approach builds on architectures like Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), which focus on predictive representations of the world, positioning AMI as a contrarian bet against labs like OpenAI and Anthropic that prioritize scaling LLMs for AGI.

The funding, co-led by investors such as Cathay Innovation and Bezos Expeditions, values AMI at $3.5 billion, with plans for global offices in Paris, Montreal, Singapore, and New York. AMI targets enterprise applications in manufacturing, biomedical, and robotics, leveraging data to build realistic world models for optimization tasks. LeCun emphasizes open-source development to prevent private control of AI, citing ethical concerns around military use and surveillance. Specific partnerships with companies like Toyota and Samsung are outlined, alongside societal impacts such as potential AI safety debates and the role of democratic oversight in technology deployment.

"The idea that you’re going to extend the capabilities of LLMs to the point that they’re going to have human-level intelligence is complete nonsense," – Yann LeCun, criticizing the LLM-centric approach to AGI.

Tony Hoare has died
⬆ 1752 pts 💬 224 HN comments

The passing of Sir Tony Hoare at age 92 marks the end of an era for computer science, as his foundational contributions continue to shape modern computing. Best known for the quicksort algorithm, with its optimal average-case O(n log n) complexity, Hoare's work extended far beyond sorting to include Hoare logic for axiomatic program verification, Communicating Sequential Processes (CSP) for concurrency modeling, and key roles in ALGOL development. These innovations established rigorous formal methods that underpin software correctness and parallel computing, influencing languages like Go and Erlang. His career, spanning from early machine demonstrations to academic leadership at Oxford and Microsoft Research, exemplified a commitment to elegant, provable solutions in an era of rapid technological evolution.

From the article, specific anecdotes such as the sixpence wager over quicksort's speed—where Hoare implemented a slower algorithm first out of professionalism—highlight his practical ingenuity. His cryptic remark on government tech supremacy ('nothing we have even comes close') sparks speculation on undisclosed advancements. Societally, Hoare's ideas permeate industry; for instance, CSP's channel-based concurrency inspired real-world systems, while his advocacy for array bounds checking, as noted in comments, emphasized safety over speed. The Hacker News community reflects this impact, with discussions on his 'billion dollar mistake' (null references) and enduring influence on formal verification, showcasing how his humility and technical depth left a lasting legacy in both education and software engineering practices.

"Nothing we have even comes close to what the government has access to. They will always be years ahead of what you can imagine." – Tony Hoare, on future tech developments, from the article.
