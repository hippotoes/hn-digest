AMD-specific fork of llama.cpp
⬆ 422 pts 💬 101 HN comments

This post is less a release note than a thesis about the fragmentation of the generalist inference engine. llama-cpp-rdna-boosts is not really a fork but a thin patchset re-applied against a pinned llama.cpp commit, carrying AMD RDNA-specific kernel work that lifts prefill and decode on a Radeon R9700 attached over an eGPU link. The author's framing is the substance: llama.cpp upstream is valued as a “stable base to branch off from,” with contributions “vetted thoroughly and applied judiciously,” whereas a 2026 wave of engines — Strata, Basalt, RustingStrata — are model- and hardware-specific artifacts largely vibe-coded by frontier models. The critique sharpens there: some of these chase tokens/sec not by optimizing kernels but by changing what the model computes — e.g. Strata preferring already-VRAM-resident experts over the routing-correct ones — trading fidelity for throughput. The patchset approach is pitched as the disciplined middle path: fewer moving parts, transparent diff, no semantic drift.

Concrete numbers: decode rises from ~35 to ~55 tok/s and prefill from ~700 to ~1300 tok/s running Swift-1.5-Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF at Q6_K_L with MTP, launched in a podman container passing /dev/kfd and /dev/dri. Commenters mapped the same terrain more broadly: gfx906 Instinct MI50s at roughly $300 for 32GB with ~1TB/s HBM2, Radeon VII Pro, llama-of-vega.cpp, llama.cpp-rdna3 for 7900XTX users, vllm-radiance, plus a claim of 2400 tok/s prefill and 150 tok/s decode on a single R9700. A llama.cpp maintainer (nikwen) noted the project now sits under NVIDIA via the Hugging Face acquisition yet insists non-NVIDIA work continues and “We're happy to take high-quality patches.” The recurring undercurrent: AMD's software investment lags its silicon, so users build the toolchain themselves — and output quality is rarely audited when speed is the headline.

“The optimizations are less about hardware-specific changes, but more about changing how the model thinks so that it goes brrr faster” — a warning that some rival engines buy throughput by perturbing expert routing rather than tuning kernels; rdna-boosts instead delivers 35→55 tok/s decode and 700→1300 tok/s prefill as a transparent patchset.

Key Highlights
  • llama-cpp-rdna-boosts is a re-applied minimal patchset against a pinned llama.cpp commit, not a divergent fork — chosen deliberately for maintainability and reviewability.
  • Measured gains on an R9700 eGPU: prefill ~700→~1300 tok/s, decode ~35→~55 tok/s on a 27B Qwen3.8 GGUF at Q6_K_L with MTP enabled.
  • The author distinguishes legitimate kernel optimization from engines that boost speed by altering model behavior (e.g. Strata preferring VRAM-resident experts over correctly-routed ones).
  • Commenters document a broader cottage industry: gfx906 MI50/Radeon VII rigs with cheap HBM2, RDNA3-specific forks, vllm-radiance, and Strix Halo APU toolboxes.
  • Upstream friction is mostly compatibility, not politics — maintainers say quality patches are welcome, but per-architecture regressions make broad merges hard.

Google Will Make AI Agents Use Its MCP Servers. The Gmail One Can't Send Email
⬆ 403 pts 💬 327 HN comments

Google's eight Workspace MCP servers went generally available on 24 November, bundled with a new Workspace AI Tools Addendum to the APIs Terms of Service. From 1 February 2027, any tool that "is or is driven by an AI Agent, an AI Harness or an AI model" must reach Gmail, Drive, Calendar and the rest of Workspace through Google's MCP servers; agents that keep hitting REST directly are quietly demoted to Standard-tier quotas regardless of what the project paid for, and the addendum explicitly forbids disguising an agent to dodge classification. The technical substance is real — the MCP gateway gives admins a chokepoint for policy checks, human confirmation and DLP, closing a genuine prompt-injection hole where a gmail.modify token let an agent act on instructions embedded in incoming mail. The implementation, however, is conspicuously asymmetric: the Gmail server exposes ten tools (search_threads, get_message, get_thread, create_draft, list_drafts, list_labels, label/unlabel) but cannot send, reply, forward, edit an existing draft, or handle attachments.

Those send and edit tools exist but "require partner access and aren't generally available" — and Anthropic's Claude Workspace connector already advertises sending, replying, forwarding and trashing Drive files, exactly the operations independent developers are denied on identical OAuth scopes. The economics reinforce the tilt: Standard tier is 5,000 MCP requests per day per project per product, with search_threads and list_drafts costing 10 each, so a search-only agent gets 500 queries a day across all its users, while the paid Scaled tier (billing from 1 April 2027) charges MCP from the first request but grants REST allowances like 70 million light Gmail requests a day. The addendum also caps retention of "Google Technical Information" — vector embeddings, memory indexes, user profiles, organisational graphs — at 30 days and bans training, fine-tuning and evals on Workspace data, which effectively outlaws long-lived inbox indexes while leaving deterministic, non-AI integrations on their own OAuth clients untouched. Classification is self-declared via a new OAuth checkbox, and clients that missed the 11 November cut-off stay permanently non-agentic.

A Gmail search_threads call costs 10 of your 5,000 daily MCP requests — meaning a search-only agent gets 500 queries a day for the entire project, while Google's own framing is that "a valid token is no longer enough."

Key Highlights
  • From 1 Feb 2027, AI-driven Workspace access must route through Google's MCP servers; direct REST use gets demoted to Standard quotas and breaches the terms.
  • Google's Gmail MCP server can search, read, draft and label but cannot send, reply, forward, edit drafts, or handle attachments — those are partner-only.
  • Anthropic's Claude connector already sends and trashes files, so parity depends on being a large vendor rather than on OAuth scope.
  • The addendum caps storage of embeddings, memory indexes and user profiles from Workspace data at 30 days and bans training/fine-tuning on it.
  • Quotas are punitive for agents: 5,000 requests/day/project/product, with search_threads costing 10, versus 70M free light Gmail REST requests/day on the Scaled tier.

Company Culture = Slack Emoji ÷ √Headcount
⬆ 516 pts 💬 264 HN comments

Liam Galvin's post proposes a deliberately absurd but analytically interesting culture metric: custom Slack emoji divided by the square root of workspace headcount, C = E_custom / sqrt(U). The square-root denominator is the key design choice: emoji are a shared resource, so a single :this-is-fine: can serve an entire department for years; linear scaling would imply a 10,000-person company should have 1,000 times the emoji of a 10-person company, which the author calls unhinged. Galvin pairs the coefficient with a participation rate, P = uploaders / users × 100, to distinguish distributed bottom-up culture from a monoculture maintained by one long-tenured backend engineer. The piece is satire, but its target is serious: engagement surveys, eNPS, mandatory fun, and HR-curated culture decks are expensive, gameable, and self-reported. Custom emoji are cheap, observable, emergent artifacts of peer interaction, and the metric inverts the usual power dynamic by measuring culture without asking employees to perform it.

The score bands are the article's comic payload. C below 5 means default emoji, a company logo, and a stale :party-parrot:; 5–20 is healthy, with a CTO-face emoji used 'supportively and as a weapon'; 20–50 is thriving, where :this-is-fine: appears over 4,000 times in #incidents; C ≥ 50 is late-stage culture, with self-referential emoji such as :old-man-yells-at-old-man: and :party-parrot-holding-a-party-parrot:. Examples from slackmojis.com include :a_big_lump_with_knobs: (corn on the cob), :austin_capmetro_bus_back:, :cat-roomba-matrix-fast:, and :cauliflower_surprise_veg_shock:. Galvin concedes gaming: a middle manager could upload 600 logo variants, and layoffs mechanically improve C. On Hacker News, 516 points and 264 comments turned the joke into a broader argument about off-topic channels, reaction latency, usage-weighted data, and Teams.

Galvin's square-root denominator is both the joke and the insight: a single :this-is-fine: is a shared institutional resource, so emoji inventories should scale sublinearly with headcount—and mass layoffs mechanically improve the score.

Key Highlights
  • The Emoji Coefficient divides custom Slack emoji by √headcount, modeling emoji as a shared resource that should scale sublinearly with org size.
  • A participation rate—percentage of users who uploaded at least one emoji—is added to detect culture concentrated in a few long-tenured employees.
  • Score bands run from default-emoji wasteland to late-stage self-referential memeology, with :this-is-fine: in #incidents as a mid-high signal.
  • Galvin explicitly acknowledges gaming via logo-variant uploads and notes that layoffs can artificially improve the score.
  • HN commenters extend the metric to off-topic channel density, reaction speed/quality, usage-weighted inventories, and Slack-versus-Teams message topology.

YouTube Ad Punisher – Auto-reloads YouTube pages when an ad plays
⬆ 502 pts 💬 601 HN comments

YouTube Ad Punisher is a Manifest V3 browser extension by George Anton that deliberately does nothing clever: when its content script detects an ad, it records video.currentTime, reloads the page via location.replace() with a &t=Xs URL parameter, and lets YouTube's own seek-on-load behaviour restore playback position. Detection is a hybrid of class polling every 500ms for .ad-showing plus a MutationObserver for skip buttons and overlay nodes, with a 3-second cooldown guard to prevent reload loops. If three reloads fail to clear the slot, it falls back to redirecting to an ad-free mirror, yout-ube.com, appending &loop=0 to dodge that site's default infinite-replay bug. It requests only storage and tabs permissions, scoped to YouTube domains, and logs every detected ad — advertiser, duration, skip-button text, video, timestamp — into a locally exportable JSON/CSV history.

The design rests on an empirical claim: YouTube's ad decisioning is largely server-side and re-evaluated per page load, so reloading is a cheap way to reroll the auction rather than defeat it — no filter lists, no network interception, nothing for anti-adblock detection to fingerprint. The README openly frames the mechanism as a 'passive protest': 'Every reload is a vote: I will not watch your ad.' That is also its weakness at scale, since each vote is a full page load. HN's 601-comment thread barely engaged with the reload trick itself; instead it bifurcated into veterans reporting uBlock Origin plus SponsorBlock on Firefox gives them literally years of ad-free viewing, and paying subscribers arguing the free tier has been engineered to be intolerable by design.

Notably, the sharpest technical critique came sideways: commenters warned that mildly popular extensions are routinely approached with acquisition offers, making any 'load unpacked' install a supply-chain decision, and one pointed at the author's separate 'ANTON-SIFTA / Alice' repository, calling it 'really quite surreal'. Others flagged dns_snek's observation that YouTube runs A/B tests over very long time frames, so 'my ad blocker works fine' and 'ads every 60 seconds' can both be true for different users — precisely the heterogeneity this extension's per-ad logging database is positioned to measure.

"Every reload is a vote: I will not watch your ad" — the extension's thesis. Each vote is a full page load, which is why the author concedes that millions of users would produce a traffic spike and an ad-impression collapse simultaneously.

Key Highlights
  • Mechanism is deliberately dumb: detect .ad-showing via 500ms polling + MutationObserver, save video.currentTime to sessionStorage, location.replace() with &t=Xs, let YouTube auto-seek back.
  • Escalation path: 3 failed reloads trigger a redirect to the yout-ube.com mirror with &loop=0 to suppress that site's infinite-replay bug; a 3-second cooldown prevents reload loops.
  • Every detected ad is logged locally (advertiser, duration, skip text, video, playback position) and exportable as JSON/CSV from the popup — the real novel artifact here is an ad-frequency dataset.
  • Permissions are minimal (storage + tabs, YouTube domains only) and MIT-licensed, but 'load unpacked' installation shifts supply-chain risk onto the user, a concern commenters raised explicitly.
  • The dominant HN objection is redundancy, not efficacy: numerous users report uBlock Origin + SponsorBlock on Firefox, LibreTube, or DNS-level geo-tricks already yield ad-free viewing without page reloads.

CTO of 'RansomwareHelp' indicted for wire fraud
⬆ 322 pts 💬 157 HN comments

KrebsOnSecurity reports the indictment of Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, 52, co-founder and CTO of Florida-based ransomwarehelp.com, on wire fraud charges returned in Rhode Island. The company's pitch was a specific technical claim: proprietary cryptographic tooling and brute-force cryptanalysis could restore 100% of encrypted data, backed by a claimed 99% success rate, 28 years of experience, and 80 experts — while explicitly promising it would "never promote, intermediate or pay ransom to threat actors." In reality, per the indictment, the firm simply contacted the ransomware operators, negotiated a decryption-key price, and resold that key to victims at a substantial markup, billing for forensic servers and engineering hours that never existed. The FBI ran a two-sided sting, posing as both a victim company with a phony ransom note and as the extortion group itself, capturing the full markup cycle in chat logs and invoices.

The mechanics matter more than the punchline. When Soteria's Paul Ihme examined a 2024 LockBit victim's network, only a single ransom note survived, recovered from a backup snapshot — suggesting RansomwareHelp used Anydesk remote access to delete notes and monopolize the negotiation channel, a claim corroborated by a LockBit chat where the firm begged the operators not to reveal that it was negotiating: "Please do not change the price. I am doing my best to achieve it." The technical claims were word salad — SHA-256 listed among "breakable encryption types," 510 preconfigured forensic servers, injected encryption codes. The case sits in a pattern: MonsterCloud's Zohar Pinhasi (allegedly $8M paid to gangs, $19M billed), Cybersteward's Edward Dubrovsky, and negotiator Angelo Martino, sentenced to 70 months for feeding BlackCat his clients' negotiating positions.

The FBI's fake ransomware crew quoted 0.2 BTC (~$13,000), settled at ~$6,000 — and Palacio billed the fake victim $16,010, a spread that his "advanced cryptographic tools" line item was invented to conceal.

Key Highlights
  • RansomwareHelp claimed proprietary cryptanalysis and a no-ransom policy while secretly purchasing decryptors and reselling them at markup
  • FBI ran a simultaneous sting as both victim and ransomware operator, documenting a ~$6,000 purchase billed at $16,010 plus a $2,750 diagnostic fee
  • A 2024 LockBit victim was quoted $438,880 for recovery involving '510 preconfigured forensic servers' and brute-force injection of encryption codes
  • Deleted ransom notes and Anydesk remote access suggest deliberate control of the victim's negotiation channel
  • Part of a DoJ pattern: MonsterCloud, Cybersteward, and negotiator Angelo Martino (70 months) for colluding with ransomware crews

Kamil Khan Mumtaz: Pakistani architect, admired at home, almost invisible abroad
Kamil Khan Mumtaz, born in Calcutta in 1939 and trained at London's Architectural Association in the early 1960s, is the subject of a Works in Progress photo essay arguing he may be the world's most under-recognised living architect — a claim that rests on a specific intellectual trajectory rather than mere novelty. Mumtaz absorbed AA modernism uncritically ('I just simply took it hook, line and sinker'), returned to Pakistan in 1966, became a communist activist imprisoned twice by the military dictatorship, and in the late 1970s produced the report that saved Lahore's Walled City from bureaucratic demolition — today a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The rupture came in 1980, when Nader Ardalan handed him The Sense of Unity: The Sufi Tradition in Persian Architecture. Mumtaz ignored it, then read it: 'It shattered all of the beliefs I had, being a Marxist, a materialist, an atheist, a modernist.' The technical substance of the subsequent decades is a revived vernacular construction system: lime rather than cement mortars and limewash tinted with natural pigment so colour lives in the substrate and walls transpire; structural brick vaulting and domes shaped to stand in pure compression,...
