DeepSeek-v4.1-Flash-1017
⬆ 888 pts 💬 667 HN comments

DeepSeek's V4.1-Flash-1017 is framed as a checkpoint refresh, yet it lands as the moment an open-weight model became many practitioners' default. The repo is a 763B-parameter multimodal decoder (image-text-to-text) under MIT, shipped at mixed precision — roughly 204B FP8-E4M3 and 557B INT8 tensors, ~510GB on disk — with a 1M-token context window and ≥256K max output. The citation title, 'Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression,' is the tell: the differentiator is memory economics, not parameter count. DeepSeek also declined to ship a Jinja chat template, providing instead a Python reference encoder (encoding.py) plus the deepseek-recipe Rust crates that normalize Messages/Chat Completions/Responses into a Conversation format covering thinking mode, numeric reasoning effort, tool calls, mid-conversation system messages, and interleaved images.

The benchmark table is honest about the 'minor update' framing: 42.6 on HLE without tools (44.0 on the text-only subset), still well behind Opus 5's 56.3, but ahead on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (42.3 vs 39.9), Apex Bench (72.1 vs 65.8), DeepSWE v1.1 (77.0), and BabyVision (92.5 with tools). The 667 comments converge on what the table cannot show — token efficiency and wall-clock latency. One commenter measured ~20% fewer tokens at every reasoning effort and a 2.5x reduction at effort level 1; others report a 40-second pelican versus 11 minutes at max. Practitioners are assembling hybrid pipelines in which Flash does the engineering and Opus 5.5 'reviews its work,' while skeptics note that single-run agent comparisons against non-deterministic models are near-worthless.

The model card's own citation names the real moat — 'Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression' — and commenters agree: the tiny KV cache makes this 'basically the only serious choice for large local models.'

Key Highlights
  • 763B-parameter MIT-licensed multimodal model, ~510GB of mixed FP8-E4M3/INT8 weights, 1M context, ≥256K max output
  • Beats predecessor on essentially every benchmark; leads Opus 5 on Terminal-Bench (42.3 vs 39.9) and Apex Bench (72.1 vs 65.8) but trails badly on HLE (42.6 vs 56.3)
  • KV-cache compression is the headline infrastructure win, enabling ~50 tok/s generation and ~3400 tok/s prefill on an M5 Ultra with 64GiB+ RAM
  • No Jinja chat template; the protocol is specified via encoding.py plus deepseek-recipe Rust libraries covering thinking mode, numeric reasoning effort, and tool calls
  • Practitioners report ~20% fewer output tokens at each reasoning level and a 2.5x reduction at effort=1, making low-effort modes finally usable

Show HN: Llmspeed – feel how fast a local LLM runs before you buy the hardware
⬆ 306 pts 💬 84 HN comments

Llmspeed.dev, a Show HN project with 306 points and 84 comments, is a browser-based simulator that lets prospective local-LLM buyers select a hardware target and model/quantization to see estimated token throughput and time-to-first-token before spending thousands on GPUs or unified-memory systems. The catalog spans RTX 3090/4090/5090, AMD Strix Halo and Radeon AI Pro, Mac Studio M-series, DGX Spark, Tesla V100, Intel Arc, CPU-only DDR5 rigs, and cloud H100/H200, paired with Qwen, Gemma, GPT-OSS, DeepSeek, GLM and Llama variants. Its central promise is intuitive: make local inference performance legible. But local LLM speed is not a single hardware property. Decode throughput is dominated by memory bandwidth and model bytes moved per token, while prefill/TTFT depends on compute, attention implementation and prompt length; KV-cache footprint, quantization format, MoE active parameters and engine-level optimizations all shift the result. The project openly labels some numbers as measured from community benchmarks and others as derived or projected from specs, which frames the core debate.

Commenters largely welcomed the purchase-planning utility but attacked the precision. Tom_alexander praised avoiding $10k gambles and Hugging Face downloads, noting a Q8 upgrade may yield only 3 more tokens/sec; thadk cited TTFT on 5,687 tokens: 36s for Gemma4 31B versus 4.7s for Gemma4 26B-A4B on an M4 Max. Yet kgeist measured 60-70 tok/s for Qwen3.6-27B 4-bit on an RTX 5090 where the site shows 46, and 100+ tok/s with ngram speculation on code; adrian17 called a Gemma 26B-A4B range optimistic; pil0u said an M2 Ultra ran nearly twice as fast as the site's M3 Ultra figure. Jabdoa2 argued real agentic coding uses 60k-140k-token prompts, yielding half to a third of benchmark decode throughput. Requests for multi-GPU, PCIe/NVLink, context length and custom tok/s expose the gap between a short-prompt simulator and production inference.

The site's own qualifier—'Estimates for a short prompt on a single device'—captures the tension: commenters report that 60k–140k-token agentic prompts can deliver half to a third of benchmark decode throughput.

Key Highlights
  • Llmspeed simulates local LLM tok/s and TTFT across GPUs, unified-memory Macs, DGX Spark, AMD Strix Halo, CPU-only rigs and cloud H100/H200, with community and projected data.
  • Positive commenters value it as a pre-purchase sanity check, especially for quantization trade-offs and avoiding massive Hugging Face downloads just to test speeds.
  • The dominant critique is that short single-device estimates ignore inference-engine tuning, speculative decoding, KV-cache behavior, context length and parallel workloads.
  • Users report concrete discrepancies: RTX 5090 46 vs 60-70 tok/s, M2 Ultra beating reported M3 Ultra, optimistic Gemma 26B-A4B ranges, and unclear KV-cache assumptions.
  • Requested additions include multi-GPU and PCIe/NVLink topology, older hardware, GPU search, custom tok/s, more quants, and context-length modeling; others suggest renting cloud GPUs or testing via OpenRouter first.

Codex Desktop continuously generates Crashpad pending dumps
⬆ 141 pts 💬 66 HN comments

OpenAI's Codex Desktop app is generating Crashpad pending dumps without bound on macOS, a defect that reveals real fragility in how AI coding agents are packaged and supervised. Issue #25921 documents that ~/Library/Application Support/com.openai.codex/web/Crashpad/pending accumulated 4.9GB across 54,504 paired .dmp/_sidecar.json files in a single day on the reporter's arm64 Mac, with counts climbing from 53,444 to 54,504 mid-investigation. Crucially, the app stayed usable, so this was not a normal foreground crash. Two browser_crashpad_handler processes dating from May 30 shared one Crashpad database, and dionylon's follow-up pinned the mechanism: the handler itself was dying in a loop with FATAL:third_party/crashpad/crashpad/handler/mac/exception_handler_server.cc:76 Check failed: kr == KERN_SUCCESS. mach_port_request_notification: (os/kern) invalid capability (20) — handing off per-user mach ports under macOS 26.3.1/26.2, apparently tied to the Chromium/Browser Use path, since disabling browser@openai-bundled and clearing BROWSER_USE_AVAILABLE_BACKENDS did not stop it.

The scale is the story: one machine reached 59GB with the filesystem approaching the 65,535-entry directory limit, another hit 63.2GB across 738,864 files (369,431 dumps) and kept minting roughly one report per second after reinstalls; a third commenter reports 330GB. The only durable mitigation is telling — making the Crashpad tree read-only and immutable (chmod -R 555 plus chflags -R uchg), which stops the growth precisely by preventing Codex from ever writing crash reports, after sudo failed to touch the signed handler inside the framework bundle. A maintainer claims later macOS builds (26.5) fix it, but versions 26.527.60818, 26.601.10930 and 26.602.71036 all show the failure. The takeaway for the agentic era: unrotated, unbounded telemetry plus a crashing Chromium subprocess turns a background diagnostic into a disk-exhaustion attack on the developer's own machine — and Codex's general habit of hoarding worktree artifacts, screenshots, and validation tests suggests this is a systemic resource-governance gap, not a one-off.

Codex's Crashpad handler was killing itself roughly once per second — one user accumulated 63GB across 738,864 files, another 330GB — and the only fix that worked was making OpenAI's crash-report directory immutable, i.e. preventing the app from ever reporting crashes at all.

Key Highlights
  • Codex Desktop's browser_crashpad_handler enters a crash loop under macOS 26.x, emitting paired .dmp/_sidecar.json files into an unrotated pending queue
  • Reported totals span 4.9GB/day, 59GB, 63.2GB (738,864 files) and 330GB, with growth continuing during investigation and after reinstalls
  • Root cause trace: FATAL exception_handler_server.cc:76, mach_port_request_notification returning 'invalid capability (20)', linked to the bundled Chromium/Browser Use path
  • Disabling browser@openai-bundled, chrome@openai-bundled and BROWSER_USE_AVAILABLE_BACKENDS had no effect; chmod 555 + chflags uchg on the Crashpad directory halted growth

Pele – Mermaid diagrams as themeable SVG
⬆ 471 pts 💬 41 HN comments

Pele is an MIT-licensed SVG renderer, published under the Obsidian org and built by kepano (Steph Ango), that consumes Mermaid syntax and emits themeable SVG with zero runtime dependencies. The context matters: Mermaid.js became the de facto diagramming DSL of the Markdown world, but its pipeline is heavy — a dagre-style layout engine plus 23 direct dependencies, fragile theming via generated `style` blocks, and a browser-shaped rendering path that is awkward in CI. Pele keeps the grammar (all diagram types in Mermaid 12.1.0) but swaps the layout engine, honoring subgraph-local direction rather than flattening it. Paint is entirely delegated to CSS custom properties — `--pele-bg`, `--pele-fg`, `--pele-line`, `--pele-surface` — so a theme switch is a cascade change, not a re-render. That separation of layout from paint is the actual architectural claim, and it is what makes dark mode and static-site embedding tractable.

The published numbers are the hook: 230 kB gzip against Mermaid's 1.6 MB, render times of 0.05–0.3 ms against 6–82 ms, and 0 versus 23 direct dependencies. But HN's 471-point response focused on layout quality, not bytes. lukeify opened with the familiar grievance — "arcane and topologically poor layout" on anything past a toy diagram — and kepano answered directly that Pele "only follows its syntax not its layout." Ancapistani called the subgraph-direction fix "sufficient reason for me to switch, even if it were slower than Mermaid.js." Elsewhere the thread turned to server-side rendering (nickmain asking whether Mermaid still needs a headless browser), competing implementations like Merman and python's mermaidx, LLM-generated diagram pipelines, and a handful of polish complaints about dark-mode examples, FOUC, and a source link that was too hard to find.

"A flowchart subgraph keeps its own direction even when it links to nodes outside the subgraph. This, in and of itself, is sufficient reason for me to switch, even if it were slower than Mermaid.js." — the performance numbers were the headline, but layout correctness was the conversion event.

Key Highlights
  • Pele reimplements Mermaid's syntax but not its layout engine, letting subgraphs retain their own direction instead of being flattened by global layout
  • Theming is done via CSS custom properties on a `.pele` scope, so color and font changes apply without re-rendering — a clean split between layout and paint
  • Claimed gains over Mermaid.js: 230 kB vs 1.6 MB gzip, 0.05–0.3 ms vs 6–82 ms render, 0 vs 23 direct dependencies
  • Supports every diagram type built into Mermaid 12.1.0, and its `render()` API returns an SVG string suitable for server-side or build-time generation
  • Built for Obsidian, MIT-licensed, hosted at github.com/obsidianmd/pele — though several users initially could not find the source

Making chicken transparent using Dorito dust
⬆ 315 pts 💬 82 HN comments

The stunt is a kitchen-table demonstration of optical clearing. Chicken breast is opaque because light scatters at countless interfaces between aqueous cytoplasm and lipid-rich membranes or organelles with mismatched refractive indices. The author uses tartrazine, the yellow food dye commonly found in Dorito seasoning, to raise the refractive index of the aqueous phase toward that of the scattering lipids—commenters cite lipid indices around 1.43–1.53—so index heterogeneity shrinks and thin sections transmit more light. That places it in the lineage of histology clearing agents such as glycerol, sucrose, fructose, and SeeDB/CUBIC, but with an intentionally absurd grocery-store source. Crucially, the result is translucency in very thin slices, not a transparent whole breast; sample thickness and surface roughness remain dominant constraints.

Commenters immediately turned to experimental craft. masfuerte suggests lightly freezing raw chicken to make thin slicing easier; _Microft proposes water between sample and grid or conducting the experiment under water to reduce surface roughness; ricardobeat suggests a sous-vide bath for controlled long cooking, and the author replies 'this is a great idea thanks.' Others mention used vibratomes under $100 and note that a 5% tartrazine solution is far more concentrated than snack residue, while gus_massa calculates that even 80% sucrose tops out near n=1.44 and cannot match lipids. The thread also drifts into food-politics: JSR_FDED calls the 'intensely chemical nature of Doritos' unsettling; augment_me rebuts with the naturalistic fallacy; the author stresses that transparency is 'just changing the refractive index of water.'

Index matching, not Dorito dust per se: tartrazine at roughly 5% raises the aqueous refractive index toward lipids (1.43–1.53), suppressing scattering in thin chicken sections.

Key Highlights
  • The effect is optical clearing via refractive-index matching: tartrazine raises the aqueous phase index toward lipid/protein scattering centers.
  • Only very thin tissue sections become translucent; cutting artifacts and surface roughness dominate the result.
  • Commenters propose freezing, vibratomes, water immersion, and sous-vide to improve sample preparation.
  • Tartrazine/Yellow 5 safety and EU labeling surface, though the author insists the effect is physical, not chemical toxicity.
  • Discussion ranges from Ig Nobel and NileRed jokes to serious interest in living-tissue imaging and histology applications.

lpviz (lpviz.net) is a browser-native interactive visualizer for linear programming, the optimization problem class — maximize cᵀx subject to Ax ≤ b, x ≥ 0 — whose simplex and interior-point solutions quietly underpin scheduling, network flow, portfolio construction, compiler register allocation, and the dual formulations behind SVMs and L1-regularized regression. What distinguishes it from the existing class of Desmos/GeoGebra-style 2D feasible-region plotters is scope: the site documents six distinct solution strategies — the Simplex Method, Interior Point Methods, PDHG (primal-dual hybrid gradient), the Ellipsoid Method, and the Central Path — inviting side-by-side comparison of algorithms that converge through fundamentally different geometry, from vertex-hopping pivot sequences to the log-barrier trajectory that cuts through the polytope's interior rather than hugging its boundary. That framing treats LP as a teaching surface for algorithm design, not merely a constraint-drawing exercise.

Gold is now the top U.S. export
⬆ 349 pts 💬 193 HN comments

For the first time in modern U.S. trade history, gold has displaced crude oil, pharmaceuticals, and aerospace as the country's single largest export category by value. According to the FRED series IEAXGG cited by Numismatic News, nonmonetary gold exports began topping all other categories in October 2025 and have done so in nearly every month since, with February 2026 tonnage potentially setting an all-time record. The directional flow is revealing: the largest share moves to Switzerland, home to the world's highest-purity refineries capable of recasting bars into LBMA- and Shanghai-acceptable kilogram formats; the second-largest goes to Hong Kong, the standard entrepôt for mainland China; and the third to the United Kingdom, seat of the London Bullion Market Association. Because the U.S. Treasury reports its official reserve stock unchanged, the outflow must be sourced from domestic mine production and scrap recycling rather than vault withdrawals.

The macro backdrop is a slow, structural rotation in reserve assets. Foreign central banks in aggregate now hold more gold by value than U.S. Treasury debt, and China's Treasury position has slid from roughly $1.096 trillion in April 2021 to about $693 billion in February 2026, with Beijing substituting bullion and promoting alternative settlement rails, including the BRICS 'unit.' Whether that explains the U.S. export surge is unproven — the author concedes 'Can I prove this? No.' — but the plumbing is consistent: Swiss refining acts as an intermediation layer between U.S. scrap/mine supply and sovereign accumulation. The HN thread supplies the missing correction: U.S. domestic mine output is only ~160 tons annually, so a record tonnage month implies the recycling channel, fueled by high prices and retail cash-for-gold liquidation, is doing far more work than the headline implies.

Foreign central banks combined now hold more gold by value than U.S. Treasury debt — and China alone has cut its Treasury holdings from $1.096T (2021) to ~$693B (Feb 2026).

  • Official U.S. gold reserves are unchanged, so flows are attributed to mine output (~160 t/yr domestically) and scrap recycling.
