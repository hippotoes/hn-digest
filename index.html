The technical foundation involves positioning a mirror to angle the built-in webcam downward, enabling it to capture finger interactions. The implementation details highlight sophisticated calibration through a user-guided process using RANSAC to compute a homography matrix, ensuring accurate mapping from webcam to screen coordinates. Specific data points include the use of 480p webcam resolution and algorithms that distinguish between touch and hover based on vertical contour distances. Societal impact is evident in the open-source release under the MIT License, fostering community experimentation and low-cost accessibility. However, as noted in the article, limitations such as lighting dependencies and the need for higher-resolution cameras for practical use underscore its proof-of-concept nature, emphasizing innovation over immediate commercial viability while challenging conventional hardware design paradigms.

Good CTE, Bad CTE
⬆ 28 pts 💬 9 HN comments

The article 'Good CTE, Bad CTE' provides a comprehensive analysis of Common Table Expressions (CTEs) in PostgreSQL, tracing their evolution from optimization fences in versions prior to 12 to more intelligent handling in modern releases. Prior to PostgreSQL 12, CTEs were always materialized, acting as barriers to query optimization by preventing predicate pushdown and index usage, which led developers to adopt workarounds like rewriting CTEs as subqueries for performance.
