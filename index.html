Show HN: Distilling DeepSeek into GPT-OSS doesn't transfer censorship. Try it
⬆ 128 pts 💬 64 HN comments

CTGT researchers investigated whether knowledge distillation from a censored frontier model transfers the teacher's political censorship along with domain capability. They distilled DeepSeek V4 Flash into GPT-OSS-120B using a hint-based, on-policy pipeline: the student's own rollout is interrupted at the first wrong step, a hint is injected, and training applies reverse-KL over the next 100 tokens. The teacher's only role is providing hints; no China-sensitive text appears in any training data. On LineageEval's 152 matched prompt pairs (76 core-political, 76 finance-adjacent), scored by four independent judge models, DeepSeek showed a +45.45 censorship gap on core-political pairs. The Flash-taught student scored -1.39, statistically indistinguishable from the untouched base, yet improved FinanceReasoning from base levels to 83.61% at an 8,000-token budget, beating Kimi K3 (81.93%) and Inkling (65.13%) at far lower inference cost.

Strikingly, a self-distillation arm in which the student wrote its own hints matched teacher-taught performance on every seed, using 12.5% fewer output tokens, so for this scoped finance task a frontier teacher added no measurable skill beyond what the base model could extract from its own corrected reasoning. The 120B model runs on a single H100/A100 in ~63 GB MXFP4 weights; the 20B release required expert-layer adaptation to avoid underfitting. At a 100,000-token budget the large frontier models regain accuracy (Kimi K3: 89.92%), but at realistic 8k workloads they truncate and fall below the 120B, inverting the model hierarchy and making a focused 120B cost-effective. The authors release LineageEval, models, and data, and explicitly note the next high-risk experiment: a Chinese-lineage base distilled from a Chinese teacher, where subliminal transfer is more plausible.

The teacher's +45.45 censorship gap vanished in the Flash-taught student (-1.39), while FinanceReasoning jumped to 83.61%—and the self-distilled model matched the teacher-taught one on every seed.

Key Highlights
  • DeepSeek V4 Flash has a +45.45 censorship gap on China-sensitive matched pairs, while the Flash-taught 120B student shows -1.39, statistically no transfer.
  • Self-distillation (hints written by the student itself) matches teacher-taught distillation on every seed, with 12.5% fewer output tokens.
  • The 120B scores 83.61% on FinanceReasoning at an 8k token budget, above Kimi K3 (81.93%) and Inkling (65.13%), at 62-160x lower cost per query.
  • LineageEval uses 152 matched prompt pairs, four judge models from four labs, and self-hosted teacher weights to avoid API moderation layers.
  • The 20B release needs expert-layer adaptation (attention-only underfits), while the 120B serves on a single H100/A100 in ~63 GB MXFP4 weights.

Gemini Robotics 2 brings whole body intelligence to robots
⬆ 562 pts 💬 448 HN comments

Google DeepMind has introduced Gemini Robotics 2, a trio of models designed as the 'intelligence layer' for next-generation robots. The flagship Gemini Robotics 2 is a vision-language-action (VLA) model that maps language and vision directly to motor commands, capable of controlling full humanoids—from walking and crouching to precise manipulation—and bi-arm systems. Gemini Robotics ER 2 is an embodied-reasoning model for multi-step planning, human-robot dialogue, and now multi-robot collaboration; it is available on Google AI Studio and Gemini Enterprise. Gemini Robotics On-Device 2 is a distilled VLA optimized for local execution, with few-shot adaptation to entirely new robot bodies using under 200 examples and a few hours of data. The authors emphasize that this moves beyond narrow table-top tasks toward general-purpose physical AI, while acknowledging that multifinger dexterity remains an open challenge.

The announcement triggered a polarized HN discussion. Broader concerns about labor displacement and capital-labor dynamics also surfaced. The release includes the ASIMOV-Agentic safety benchmark and on-device execution to address connectivity and privacy, but overall the community sees this as an impressive GPT-1/GPT-2 moment rather than a finished product.

New bi-arm robot embodiments can be adapted in a few hours, typically with less than 200 examples — a striking leap from prior robot-specific training.

The Economic Benefit of Refactoring
⬆ 243 pts 💬 103 HN comments

Giles Edwards-Alexander, a Thoughtworks CTO, describes an experiment in which an entire 150,000-line application was written by AI coding agents (mostly Claude Code) and then systematically refactored to measure the economic payoff. The target was the data-access layer: a single 17,155-line Rust file that had grown without human review. Using a strict, test-preserving sequence of refactorings, he repeatedly asked a fresh sub-agent to implement the same new feature (an ItemWatchStore trait) and recorded token counts after each step. The headline result: input tokens for that identical change dropped from 159,564 to 27,360, an 83% reduction. Importantly, the data-access layer's total line count stayed roughly constant, so the savings came from better modularization and structure—letting the agent read fewer files—not from deleting code.

At Sonnet 5 pricing the per-change saving is only about $0.40, a deliberately modest figure; the real argument is that every future change touching that layer inherits the saving, and that compact files improve the model's ability to reason about correct edits. The experiment also exposed limits of current tools: Claude was "not good at refactoring"—it happily wrote 17k-line files, missed the single most valuable decomposition, and had to be guided by a human using Python scripts for mechanical edits. The author estimates the refactoring cost at an upper bound of 5M tokens over eight hours, but did not accurately capture it. Commenters connected this to Fowler's refactoring legacy, prompting debates about whether LLMs make refactoring more or less important, and whether token economics are the right lens for code health.

Input tokens for the same feature change fell from 159,564 to 27,360—an 83% reduction—while the data-access layer's total line count barely moved. The win came from structure, not deletion.

The research estimates roughly 38,000 boxes phoned home to a single expired domain, suggesting the full botnet is far larger, with daily ad-fraud revenues around $50,000. The fraud loop is technically sophisticated: the malware combines vision and reasoning systems to identify ads, navigate pages, and mimic human browsing well enough to fool ad networks.
