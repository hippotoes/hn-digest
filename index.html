#1
Kimi K3.1 Pro and K3.1 Pro Highspeed
⬆ 948 pts 💬 568 HN comments

Moonshot AI's Kimi K3.1 Pro is a post-trained derivative of K3 rather than a new base model — the headline claim is not architectural but infrastructural: a 3T-class open-weight model with a 1M-token context window, produced by scaling post-training compute across "hundreds of downstream domains" via a jointly-optimised RL stack spanning rollouts, sandboxing, long-context inference, and environment quality checks. Kimi is explicit that the win came from making each increment of RL compute pay predictable returns, which is the same thesis every frontier lab is now pursuing: the base model is a commodity, and the marginal capability lives in environment engineering and reward infrastructure. A companion "Highspeed" variant trades nothing but latency for 2–3× throughput, signalling that inference economics, not raw IQ, is now the competitive axis.

The numbers are aggressive: 65.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (ahead of GPT-6 Astra's 58.2%, behind Claude Opus 5.5's 66.4%), 92.7% on BenchCAD with tools, 64.4 on OfficeQA Pro v2, and an internal hallucination rate cut from 23.4% to 8.3% on Kimi Hallu Bench Hard. The most consequential section is the cybersecurity one: Moonshot found cyber capability rising as a byproduct of broad mid-training and RL data, then deliberately stripped cyber-related data from both stages, dropping xdev-bench-hard pass@3 from 90.5% to 81.0% in the shipped open weights. Pricing fell to $2/$10 per MTok despite the larger model. Weights land by Nov 4, with an implicit argument that gradual capability diffusion beats concentration.

Moonshot publishes the control experiment most labs would bury: an unstripped checkpoint solves 90.5% of xdev-bench-hard cyber-exploitation tasks at pass@3, while the released K3.1 Pro is deliberately throttled to 81.0% — a rare, quantified admission that released capability is a policy choice, not a ceiling.

Key Highlights
  • K3.1 Pro is a post-trained K3 with much larger post-training compute; the claimed novelty is a joint RL stack across hundreds of domains, not a new base architecture.
  • 3T parameters, 1M-token context, weights promised by Nov 4; Highspeed variant trades latency for 2–3× speed at identical pricing.
  • Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 65.7% beats GPT-6 Astra (58.2%) and narrowly trails Claude Opus 5.5 (66.4%); internal Kimi Code Bench 3.0 Long-horizon rises from 55.95% to 61.2%.
  • Cyber-related data was identified and removed from both mid-training and RL stages, reducing offensive capability by ~9.5 points on xdev-bench-hard while retaining general coding ability.
  • API pricing drops to $2/$10 per MTok (from $3/$15) with $0.20 cached reads — but subscription plans draw heavy complaints about 5-hour quota exhaustion.

Today, we hacked what we'd set out to hack: the iPhone
⬆ 930 pts 💬 600 HN comments

Calif — the vulnerability research outfit founded by Thai Duong (co-discoverer of BEAST and CRIME, later of Wycheproof fame) and staffed with names like lcamtuf, Tavis Ormandy, Dion Blazakis and Stefan Esser — used a Hexacon Paris keynote to drop four exploit demos rather than a paper: a 1-click via Signal, an iMessage chain, a root shell on an iPhone SE3 running iOS 27.0.1, and a closing slide captioned "APT stands for Apple Persistent Threat," which commenters read as a persistent, possibly untethered compromise. The blog post itself is deliberately teaser-shaped: no CVE numbers, no technical writeup, four numbered hints and a promise that "everything will be released in due time." Duong (HN user cryptbe) confirmed in-thread that most bugs in these chains have already been disclosed to Apple and the remainder will be, that the firm reported "close to 100 bugs to Apple" in 2026, and that it does not accept Apple bounties.

The technical conversation converged on chain architecture rather than the punchline. Commenters reconstructed the shape of such work as WebKit/parser bug → sandbox escape → KASLR bypass → kernel execution, echoing the Project Zero ForcedEntry analysis of Pegasus, and stressed that parser compromise is the cheap part: "how did they break out of the app sandbox?" as creata put it. Others read the "do not update" instruction as evidence the surface is userland, not SecureROM — aseipp noted that subverting the boot chain would imply compromising the Secure Enclave, and that "truly untethered (persistent) jailbreaks haven't been a thing for 10 years." PDF resurfaced as the perennial vector — an executable format with JavaScript, image decoders and legacy compressors — with the community split on whether Lockdown Mode, which blocks message attachments, meaningfully mitigates these. The AI subtext ran throughout: whether LLM-assisted bug discovery is now an insider moat rather than a public good, and whether defenders can find the same bugs just as fast.

"So far in 2026, we've reported close to 100 bugs to Apple. We don't accept Apple bounties" — Thai Duong, in-thread, on a team whose four demos include a root shell on the current iOS release and something he calls "Apple Persistent Threat."

Key Highlights
  • Calif (Thai Duong's firm, with lcamtuf, Tavis Ormandy, Dion Blazakis, Stefan Esser) demoed 1-click Signal, iMessage, root-shell and persistent-compromise chains at Hexacon Paris
  • Technical detail is withheld; full writeups promised over coming weeks, with most chain bugs already reported to Apple
  • Commenters reconstruct the typical chain as parser/bug → sandbox escape → KASLR bypass → kernel execution, noting sandbox escape is the hard part
  • PDF is repeatedly identified as the durable attack surface — executable semantics, JavaScript, and legacy image decoders defeat memory-safe rewrites
  • Debate over whether LLM-accelerated vulnerability discovery widens access or entrenches an inner-circle moat at frontier labs

#3
Apple Music 404 Not Found
⬆ 804 pts 💬 241 HN comments

Apple has turned a dead URL into a piece of interactive corporate archaeology. Hitting music.apple.com/this-url-doesnt-exist returns a 404 page rendered as a functional click-wheel iPod: a skeuomorphic scroll wheel you drag and rotate, a backlit LCD with the characteristic edge light-bleed, the mechanical click on each detent (toggleable in settings), and a working Now Playing screen. The design is a direct homage to the fourth/fifth-generation iPod UI, reproduced with enough fidelity that commenters immediately noticed the subtlety of the backlight gradient. Under the hood it is not a video or canvas trick so much as a small web application: playlist data is fetched per-territory from static JSON endpoints under /includes/ipod/data/{territory}/{locale}.json, with the file's favicon still carrying the iOS 7-era iTunes logo. The set list is period-accurate to Apple's own mid-2000s iPod commercials and Apple Music decade playlists, including the accidental duplicate-title artifacts (Fall Out Boy's 'Thnks Fr Th Mmrs') that make it feel like a real ripped library rather than a curated marketing asset.

The deeper story is the resurrection of a famously undiscoverable Easter egg: Settings > About, hold the center button for five seconds, and you get a Breakout clone — the same gesture that hid a game on real iPods twenty years ago. Discovery-driven design is rare from Apple at this scale, and the HN thread treats the artifact as both nostalgia object and interface critique. The click wheel was a one-dimensional, eyes-free, high-precision input device whose closest modern analogue (car infotainment scroll wheels) commenters describe as nowhere near the same polish. The page also exposed implementation seams: browsers that deep-link into Apple Music block the page entirely, and the playlist turns out not to be personalised from your library — 1,029 songs and 682 artists for the US, 1,217 for France — which disappointed users expecting their own listening history. Multiple users bypassed the UI entirely and queried the JSON directly.

A 404 page is now one of the most thoughtfully engineered surfaces Apple ships: a working click wheel, a hidden Breakout game behind Settings > About, and per-region playlist JSON (1,029 US tracks vs 1,217 for France) served from a static endpoint.

Key Highlights
  • The 404 page is a functional iPod click-wheel emulator, complete with detent clicks, backlight unevenness, and a Now Playing screen
  • The classic Breakout Easter egg survives: Settings > About, hold the center button ~5 seconds
  • Playlist data is served per-territory as static JSON at /includes/ipod/data/{territory}/{locale}.json, queryable directly
  • Safari deep-links the URL into the Apple Music app, blocking the page unless the user long-presses or opens a new tab
  • Commenters note the wheel beats 'scrollscrollscroll... too far... back' manual binary search on long lists, and that period-accurate playlists follow iPod commercial-era music

CVE Tracker
⬆ 180 pts 💬 69 HN comments

A single-page, openly 'vibe coded' dashboard at cve.0xosprey.com — built after the historically used CVE trend site went down — plots daily, weekly, and cumulative counts of CVE identifiers drawn from the CVEProject/cvelistV5 repository, with a projection line comparing current pace against the median day-of-month fraction of the trailing twelve months. The chart's striking feature is not the absolute number (roughly 12,000 records a month, with one commenter counting 79,764 JSON files for the current year in the source repo) but the shape: a visibly compounding curve that has drawn attention because it coincides with a wave of automated vulnerability discovery. The visualization is thin — modes, a log-scale toggle, CSV export, PNG copy — but the underlying data pipeline is the real story: bulk git-cloned CVE records, aggregated client-side, no severity weighting by default.

The engineering audience immediately reframed the graph. The sharpest objection is Goodhart's law: GitHub became a CNA in mid-2024 and the Linux kernel in mid-2025, both explicitly lowering the cost of filing, and kernel maintainers acknowledge that 'almost any bug might be exploitable' given the kernel's position in the stack. A developer's >20k-download Obsidian plugin received a CVE over a pathing regex on a beta build — an outcome one commenter says 'would have never happened 2 years ago.' Others argue the trend survives severity filtering: critical-rated CVEs reportedly run ~25x 2020 levels against ~5x for all CVEs, though CVSS's unencoded 'vulnerable component' choice makes cross-context severities weakly comparable. The practical consequence is visible in maintainer behavior: refusing bug reports that lack a mitigation patch, and reading the axis as 'vulnerability removals.'

The most quoted reframing: 'Looks like an exponential curve... but this is just "CVEs got Goodharted"' — a filing-count artifact amplified by GitHub (CNA since mid-2024) and the Linux kernel (since mid-2025), not necessarily a 5x growth in exploitable risk.

Key Highlights
  • Cumulative CVE publication counts are compounding, with roughly 12,000 new IDs per month and tens of thousands of JSON records per year in cvelistV5
  • GitHub and the Linux kernel becoming their own CNAs in mid-2024 and mid-2025 lowered filing friction and mechanically inflated counts
  • Severity-filtered views are contested: one cited dataset shows critical CVEs at ~25x 2020 levels versus ~5x for all CVEs, but CVSS scope ambiguity undermines comparison
  • Commenters report real-world abuse: CVEs filed to force internal upgrades, CVEs on beta releases, and low-quality AI-assisted reports that maintainers now refuse without patches
  • The site itself drew criticism for a broken log-scale toggle that freezes the page, and for an everything-in-one-file LLM-generated codebase

#6
Air Purifiers as White Noise Machines
⬆ 145 pts 💬 132 HN comments

Jeff Kaufman's post makes a deceptively simple engineering argument: the white-noise machine is a fan in a box that wastes its airflow, so replace it with a fan that pushes air through a HEPA filter and get sound masking and particulate removal from the same watts. The acoustic rationale is sound — cheap electronic noise machines rely on small drivers that cannot reproduce low frequencies, whereas a purifier's fan, baffled by pleated filter media and plastic housing, produces broadband low/mid-frequency 'whoosh' much closer to pink noise. Kaufman's canonical example is a $47, 6.5-inch unit rated 34 CFM CADR at 45 dB; his own AirFanta 3Pro moves roughly 6x that air at equal noise (or ~12x at ~56 dB) for $165 and a bulkier, uglier 12.6-inch footprint. The framing is marginal-value: the second function is nearly free once you have decided to make noise anyway.

The quantitative core is a single-room mass-balance calculation. In a 10ft x 10ft room with an 8ft ceiling and a typical 0.5 ACH leakage rate, a 34 CFM purifier delivers enough clean air to cut indoor particulate concentration to roughly 16% of outdoor levels — since AQI is nonlinear, that maps to AQI 200 dropping to about 72, or 100 to 31. That is a real health delta for wildfire smoke and infectious aerosols, not a placebo. The comments extend the analysis in several directions: acoustic spectrum preferences (DOHM fan units, box fans, pink over white noise), power draw (Kaufman cites an AirFanta at 141 CFM for under 5W, undercutting claims of 50-100W), directed laminar-flow 'personal' purifiers like the AirFanta 4Lite, and measurement hygiene — one commenter's PM10 reading below PM2.5 drew the tart correction that you cannot have more people under 2.5 years old than under 10.

A 34 CFM purifier in a typical 10x10 room at 0.5 ACH cuts indoor particulates to ~16% of outdoor levels — the AQI equivalent of going from 200 to 72, for $47 and 45 dB of noise you wanted anyway.

Key Highlights
  • Fans in boxes produce better white noise than small speakers because filter media and housings low-pass the fan's high-frequency content, yielding a deep whoosh that cheap drivers cannot replicate
  • Small purifiers (~34 CFM CADR at 45 dB, $47) trade cleaning power for size and price; high-CADR units like the AirFanta 3Pro move 6-12x the air at equal or higher noise
  • Room-scale math: 0.5 ACH leakage plus 34 CFM filtration drops indoor PM to ~16% of outdoor concentration, equivalent to AQI 200 → 72
  • Efficiency claims matter — an AirFanta on setting 2 draws under 5W at 141 CFM, deflating the 'purifiers waste energy vs. a speaker' objection to roughly $10/year
  • Adjacent design space includes laminar-flow personal purifiers, silent low-speed operation for daytime filtration, and dual-use furniture like IKEA's FÖRNUFTIG table
